## The rules as built

1. **Peer's own words are explained in the paper's context.** `peerWordsSource(body.source)` (`lib/papers/explain.ts`): only a literal "summary" or "answer" counts; absent, "paper", any other word or a non-string is a paper passage. For Peer's words the route does not call `locatePassage`; a passage that clips to nothing is 422. `buildExplainPrompt` / `buildExplainReplyPrompt` take a `PassageFrame` (`located` xor `peerWords`): for Peer's words the user prompt carries `passageFrom` ("Peer's own words about this paper: a line of the summary Peer wrote of it" / "part of an answer Peer gave the reader about it", "They are not the author's words"), the passage (clipped to 1,200), the same bounded `paper` (title, abstract) and `sections` (heading + opening) as a paper passage, and no `context`. The first answer keeps "What it means" and "Why it is here" (why Peer's words say it about this paper); a quote is asked for only from a section's `opening` (the paper's own words) and held to the whole paper by the unchanged verifier — unverifiable or absent: `here.peer = true`, no quote. The reply prompt is the same for a thread (thread caps, detail, search rule as before). No table: `sanitizeExplainAnswer` / `sanitizeExplainReply` take `{ items: false }` (a row has no paragraph of the paper to be held to), and the prompt does not offer one. `explainCacheKey(…, peerWords)` appends `|peer:<source>`: the same words in the paper, the Summary and an answer are three entries; a paper passage's key is what it was. A passage with no source mark that is not in the paper is still 422.
