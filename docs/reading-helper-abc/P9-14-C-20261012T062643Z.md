## 2. The rule, as built (`lineOf`; `SUBSCRIPT`, `SUBSCRIPT_HEAD`, `SUBSCRIPT_WHOLE`, `subscriptLength`)

A **lowered group** (the consecutive lowered runs P9-13b already judges as one, a stored space run among them) is written with U+2080–U+208E and U+2093 when all of these hold:
- its text, with the white space between the characters removed, opens with a head made only of `0–9 + − - = x`, **with a digit among them**, and the head does not end on a sign;
- what follows the head is nothing, or a bracketed argument the page lowers too: the head alone is converted and the argument stays as P9-13b writes it (`I` + `0 (hkl)` is `I₀(hkl)`; the real page stores `"0 ("`, `hkl`, `)`), and when the whole group is digits, signs, x and balanced brackets it is converted whole (`T₁₍₂₎`, `Ca₃(PO₄)₂`, whose second `)` is a base);
- it stands on a formula: the last run written is at the line's size, ends on a letter, `)` or `]` (never on a space), and the group starts within 0.2 em of that run's edge (a stored space run 0.15 pt wide does not part them, a word space does). The stored space is dropped; no space is put before the group or between its characters; the space behind it stays.

`CoO₂⁻` follows from P9-08's rule (the charge is a raised run, `⁻`); `CITED` now reads a subscript digit as a digit (`[\p{L})][\d₀-₉]+$`), so a citation number after `LiCoO₂` is still dropped (without that it became an exponent). A subscript group takes the place of P9-13b's label rule: `y` + `1−x` is `y₁₋ₓ`, not `y(1-x)`.

Left as P9-13b writes it (`A1g`, `RCT`, `TH`, `Eg`, `jmax`, `f(area)`, `Ni0.5`, `Figure 2`): any group with another letter or a point; **a group that ends on a sign** (`Ti` + lowered `3+`, `Fe` + lowered `4−`: a charge set low, not a stoichiometry; it keeps P9-08's pinned `Ti3+`); a group a word space from its base, after a digit, after a space, at a line's head, after a raised run; and plain text.

**Two places I read the brief narrowly, and why**
1. *The letter `x` counts only inside a group that has a digit* (`1−x`, `1+x`). A lowered `x` on its own (`Lix`, `Nix`) stays `Lix`. Read literally the brief would write `Liₓ`; on the owner's paper that gave `LiₓCoO2` in seven lines (the `2` is not a run of its own there, see §6), a mixed form that reads worse than `LixCoO2`, and it would have broken P9-13b's surviving guard `Li` + lowered `x` → `LixCoO2`. One line (`/^(?=[+−=x-]*[0-9])…/` in `SUBSCRIPT_HEAD`) changes it if you want the literal rule.
2. *A group ending on a sign is not converted* (above). The brief's set has `+ −` for `Li₁₊ₓ`, `Li₁₋ₓ`; a trailing sign after digits is a charge.
