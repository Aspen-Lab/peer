## The rules and my choices

1. **The word under the line stays under it, to within a line.** A character anchor (a text node and an offset) read by `caretPositionFromPoint` (`caretRangeFromPoint` where that is what the browser has), and put back by its one-character `Range` rect at the same y, by the same one instant `scrollBy` in the column's observer (after layout, before paint, one a frame).
   - **x (asked for in the brief):** the middle of the **paragraph's own box** (`rect.left + width / 2`), then 30 % and 70 % of it where the middle hits a formula or other non-text. Why the middle: it is the one point of a line that no word of the line is more than half a line's width from, so a line that re-wraps moves the other words of it the least. Why the paragraph's box and not the column's: the paragraph is what holds the text (a rewrite beside it narrows it). The caret is read once when the middle is text (a test holds that).
   - **y (my choice, to report):** `CARET_LINE_PX` = `PLACE_LINE_PX` + 6 = 62, about the middle of the first line clear of the masthead (foot 48; a line is about 29 px), so the line is at least half in view. Reading at 56 exactly would take a line mostly hidden under the masthead. 62 is the y at which the walk's own probe reads "the word under the masthead's foot". The paragraph's fraction / gap is still read at `PLACE_LINE_PX` (56), as before.
   - **What is kept:** the share of the character's own box that is above the line (so a text step, which scales the box about the line, scales it, to within a tenth of the glyph).
   - **Fallbacks, to the paragraph's fraction / gap as it was:** the paragraph starts below the line (the gap; no caret is even asked for); the caret's text is a formula, a figure, a control or screen-reader text (`.katex, .katex-display, math, code, figure, button, svg, .sr-only`); the character is in another paragraph than the one at the top, or not under the line (half a box either way); a collapsed / hidden character (box under 6 px); the node has left the page (`isConnected`, no box); the browser has neither caret function.
2. **Everything P11-45 holds, still holds:** reads only at the reader's own scrolls (a test holds that the correction's own scroll event is not read); the 600 ms hold; nothing at scroll 0 or the foot; instant scrolls; every cause with no per-cause code; the cards, the map, the Back-to-paper return and the arrivals untouched. `keep-place.test.ts` (38) and the shell's flow test (23) are green with one re-aimed line, named below.
3. **No jank:** one `scrollBy` a frame (a 60-frame drag test), the correction after layout and before paint as before. Cost measured below.
4. **(rule added by the manager) The reader's scroll during the settle re-anchors; it does not end the keeping.**
   - The reader's scroll made while the settling is on reads the anchor again at their place and leaves the hold on (`settleUntil` is not zeroed).
   - While they are scrolling (a scroll of theirs in the last `READER_SCROLL_MS` = 150) a correction that would go **against the direction they went** is not made; the place is read again from where it stands, so no later jump back. The direction is kept by time, not reset by the layout key: a text step's 0.3 s transition changes the column's width at every frame, so each of its frames is a change of key.
   - A scroll the window has made that its event has not yet told (the observer is told before the event) is the reader's too: it sets the direction, and the correction is the change of the heights only, **not** the heights less their scroll (so their scroll is not undone). A move that ends at the foot is the browser's clamp for a page that became too short, not the reader's, and is put right as ever (found by P11-45's own tests, which failed without this).
   - Once they have stopped (150 ms), the keeping goes on both ways until the settling is over.
