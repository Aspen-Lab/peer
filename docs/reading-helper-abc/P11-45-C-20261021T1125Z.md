## The defect, and the rule (ruling §1h.146)

At 1280 x 720, scrolled to 8,000, a left drag of +60 re-flowed the column 552 to 492 px and moved the paragraph at the top by one; a margin drag of -80 (552 to 472) by two; a collapse or a pin re-flows 552 to 646. The browser keeps the scroll **offset**; the paragraphs above the reader grow or shrink; the words under the eye are other words. I reproduced it on the base on an invented paper (below) at 1280 x 720 and 1093 x 614 (drags, keys, collapse, pin) and at 1920 x 1080 (Fit on and off, a resize, a text step); at 1440 and 1920 a drag moves only the gutter, the column being capped. Nothing in the page held the place; `paragraphAtTop` (the Back-to-paper return's reading) was only used there.

**The rule, as built — one sentence: the point of the paper under the masthead's foot stays under it.**

1. **The same paragraph stays at the top, and the same line of it.** The place is `paragraphAtTop(document, 56)` (the Back-to-paper return's own call, `return-to-paper.ts` unchanged; 56 = `DOCK_TOP_PX + 8`, its own default) and **how far down that paragraph the line at y = 56 falls, as a fraction of the paragraph's height** (`fraction = (56 - top) / height`). After a re-flow the paragraph is put at the `top` that gives the same fraction of its *new* height above the line (`shiftToKeep`). So inside a long paragraph the line nearest the top stays the nearest line: the same fraction into it is the same line to within a line (invented paper: a 1,559-px paragraph, held to 0.1 to 0.5 px). A paragraph that starts **below** the line (the 16 px between two) keeps its gap in px instead. When it applies: whenever the text column's **layout key** changes — its width (tenths of a px, in window px), `--reading-scale` and Fit's `--page-zoom` — which covers a separator drag, its keys, a collapse and a pin, Fit on and off, the text steps and a window resize with no per-cause code. A change of height alone (the reading arriving, a block opening) is not a re-flow and is left as it was.
2. **The top and the foot are left alone.** The place is `null` — nothing is held, nothing is scrolled — at `scrollY < 1`, at the page's foot (to within a pixel), with no paragraph on the screen (the Summary tab), and with the paragraph at the top starting below the window.
3. **No jank.** **A drag re-anchors on each frame, not at the end and not at each pointer move.** Why: a divider already writes its width once per animation frame (`pane-divider.tsx`, unchanged), and the correction is made in the **column's `ResizeObserver` callback** — after that frame's layout and before its paint — by one `scrollBy({ top, behavior: "instant" })`; so the frame the reader sees is the corrected one. Re-anchoring only at the end would leave the text re-wrapping under a place that has moved thousands of px (the base's drag moves the place by up to 1,747 px) and then jump at the release; re-anchoring at each pointer move would scroll 2 to 3 times per frame for nothing. The place is read at the reader's **own** scrolls only (a correction's scroll event is known by the position it leaves and not read), and kept as it was read through every frame, so sixty corrections a second all go back to the one place and do not drift on each other's rounding. For `SETTLE_MS = 600` after a re-flow the place is held through the page's own settling: Fit and the text steps are a 0.3 s transition (`.zoom-transition`: zoom, widths, font sizes), so heights go on changing for some frames with the key unchanged; the reader's own scroll ends the hold at once. No smooth scroll anywhere (`behavior: "instant"`), so reduced motion has nothing to respect (measured with `reducedMotion: reduce`).
4. **Nothing else changes.** Pane widths, the map's `scrollTop`, the Back-to-paper return and the arrivals are untouched (measured, below); an open card follows its paragraph as before (better: the paragraph no longer runs away).

**How the release resolves once the pointer passes through (the manager's question).** The rule of the notes tab was applied to both separators first. It does pass the pointer through — while a button is held, `document.elementFromPoint` over the separator returned `div.reader-shell` instead of `div.reader-divider` — but **what lies under a separator is the shell's grid itself**: the separator is a 12-px track between the centre's cell and the margin's (or the left pane's), with no text behind it. The browser resolves a point in a text-less block to its place in the document, and the selection ended at **the same character count in every cell**, no buttons. (The notes tab worked because under *its* strip lay the centre's own box.) So the rule was taken out and not committed, and the separators keep the pointer and put the selection's end right themselves: `endSelectionInColumn` in `pane-divider.tsx`. While a press made elsewhere is held over a separator (a mouse, a button held, no drag of the separator's own) and at its release, once per animation frame — after the browser's own move of the end, before the paint — a selection that began in the paper's column is extended to the column's nearest edge at the pointer's height (`caretPositionFromPoint`, else `caretRangeFromPoint`), which is the line's end. The release then lands where the pointer is clamped: the selection is the line and the toolbar's `selectionchange` offers its buttons. Tried and measured too: correcting at `mousemove` (the browser's own default runs after it and wins) — the correction has to come after, in a frame.

**Confirmed after** (`h/divider-check.mjs`): both separators are 12 px wide (320 and 908); a left drag of +60 re-flows 552 to 492 and selects nothing; focus lands on the left separator and moves nothing (`scrollMoved` 0); ArrowRight x3 then End then Home move `aria-valuenow` to 380 and 240; the 21 tests hold the same. The place rule was re-measured on the final tree (1280 x 720, y8000 and mid, left +60, margin -80, End, collapse: drift at most 0.4 px).

## Unsettled / for the manager

1. **The notes tab's rule does not carry over to the separators.** See above: the measurement is in the table; the separators' own clamp does the work. The same clamp does **not** reach a release farther out: 40 or 100 px past a line's end (over `aside.reader-margin`) still ends at the end of the paper (36,580 characters, no buttons) and 40 px before a line's start (over the left pane) at 20,641. Those are neither separator. If wanted: the function takes a grid and a point, so one document-level listener (a press that began in the column, the pointer outside it) would cover the margin and the pane with the same call; it is not mine to add (`reader-shell.tsx` is the hook's call only) and I did not.
2. **`.reader-divider-rest`** (the inert 12-px div the shell draws in place of the left separator when the pane is a rail) is in `reader-shell.tsx`; a release before a line's start over it would end the selection wrongly in the same way. Not measured, not changed.
3. **One column (under 1024 px) is not covered:** the shell is not mounted there, so a window resize (a phone turned) keeps the offset as before; `reader-layout.tsx` and `page.tsx` were not mine.
4. **The place is held only for a paragraph on the screen.** With no body paragraph at the top (the Summary tab), or one starting below the window, the page is left to the browser: a re-flow there keeps the offset as it always did. A figure or a long heading between the line and the next paragraph is kept as that paragraph's gap in px, not as the figure's own place.
5. **Scroll anchoring does not hold the place on this page** (Chromium): not through the width change and not through a 300-px block put above the reader (the keeper's scroll suppressed; `h/anchor.mjs`). My fix commit's header said anchoring "stands down when the width of the anchor's ancestors changes", which I had not measured; commit `68d6259d` replaces it with what was measured. I did not find *why* anchoring is inactive here (no `overflow-anchor` in the CSS).
6. **"Painted" is a sampling, not a screenshot:** the number comes from a ResizeObserver created after the page's own, which the spec runs after the page's callback and before the paint. I did not capture composited frames. The animation-frame sampling shows the same cases at hundreds of px for a key, a collapse or a pin because it reads before the frame's observer step.
7. **A text step on the Paper digestion tab scales nothing** (`text-title`, above); that is outside this work and the owner may already know. The Summary tab has no body paragraphs, so a step there is not a place to keep.
8. **The hold (600 ms)** also holds the place if the reading arrives within 600 ms of a re-flow (the same words stay under the eye); the reader's own scroll ends it. It is not a flag, and nothing is kept per user.
