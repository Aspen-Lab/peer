**Rule 2: the dividers' room.** `reader-shell.tsx` gives both `PaneDivider`s `room={{ viewport, other, centreMinimum: centreDragMinimum }}`: `aria-valuemax`, End, the arrow keys' clamp and a pointer drag all end at the drag floor, never below it. **Rule 3: the default layout does not change; a saved width may take the centre to the drag floor.** `fitPanes` takes `centreDragMinimum` beside `centreMinimum` (the budget); `reader-shell.tsx` passes both.
- With **no width saved** (`left` / `margin` undefined; the pin alone is not a width) the answer is the old one, **byte for byte**: the same arithmetic, now in `squeeze()`, the budget as the floor, the margin first and then the left pane.
- **Which layout stands is the budget's question.** The left pane is a column or the rail by the budget, whatever was saved: a saved width never turns a rail into a pinned pane. (A variant that decided the structure at the drag floor would flip the rail to a pinned pane when a margin is dragged out at 1280 x 720, largest step, and give the margin back; mutation 3, "a saved width decides rail or pinned", below is that variant, and it is red.)
- **As the rail** only the margin is on the page: its floor is the drag floor if the margin was saved, the budget if only the left width was (a width the page does not show takes nothing). So 853 with only a left width saved still shows the margin at 281.
- **Pinned, with a width saved** (and a drag floor under the budget): the floor is the drag floor, and the pane the reader did not set gives way first, so a dragged pane is the last to give back what it was dragged to. **That pane is wanted no wider than it was before** (`max(its first-visit width, the budget's answer with the saved width)`): see the follow-up below.
- A drag floor that is absent, not a number, or not under the budget is no drag floor: the old answer, saved widths or not.

*The follow-up (`9276dc5f`), what I found and why.* My first cut (`c8890d84`) wanted the unsaved pane back at its default as soon as any width was saved. The owner's laptop at 125 % is 1093 css px, where the first visit squeezes the left pane to 289 (left 289, margin 280, centre 500); the first cut gave left 320 and centre 469 when the margin was committed where it already was (280), and left 320, margin 300, centre 449 for a 20 px drag (the final rule: 289 and 480). I found it with a sweep of 2.2 million drag cases against the old fit (and with a real mouse drag at 1100 and 1366 in Chromium: left 296 to 320, 276 to 320), then fixed it. The properties now held by tests and by that sweep, at every window from 853 to 1920, every step: committing the width a pane is shown at changes nothing; widening a pane leaves the other where it was; narrowing never shrinks it; a dragged pane is kept (at the end of its range, in the middle, at the least); a saved pane never has less than the budget alone gave it; the structure never flips; the centre is never under the drag floor.
