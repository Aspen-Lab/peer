## 2. Where the time went, and the rule as built

**Not where the brief guessed.** The brief named the `body()` fixture, `splitSentences` and the routing. Measured in the test's own process (V8 profiler through `node:inspector`, 100 µs sampling, over 144 routes on the short body with the old `variant${n}` questions: the 72 of "is per body" and 72 more, uncached): `termVariantMatches` 629 ms + `termOccurrences` 594 ms of 1,404 ms (87 %) — both in `lib/scoring/term-expand.ts`, both build a `RegExp` with `\p{L}\p{N}\p{M}` look-arounds on every call; `canonicalize` 31 ms; `routeSection` 15 ms; `splitSentences` 7.5 ms. Alone, `splitSentences` over the whole body is 0.09 ms and `buildReading` is 1 ms. The fixture is small (6 sections, 6 paragraphs, 60 sentences, 7.4 kB).

Two costs, both per question:

1. **A term the process has not routed before.** `typed(n)` was `graph embeddings protein structure variant${n} cathode`, a new term (`variant0` … `variant71`) in every question. Per route, with the body cut to a hundred characters so the body is not the cost: **about 0.5 ms for the first 24 questions and 8–13 ms from the 26th on, a step**. The 25 first texts had been routed (their patterns built) by the test before it in the file, "holds a bounded number" (which routes `typed(0)` … `typed(24)`); the 26th is the first term nobody had seen. The same step is gone once `typed(n)` has one set of terms (routes 25 and 26: 0.43 and 0.51 ms; the later ones 0.4 to 3 ms on a loaded machine). I did not separate `new RegExp` from the match in the profiler (both are inlined into the two functions above), so "compiling a Unicode-class pattern" is the reading the step supports, not a separate measurement.
2. **The match over the body.** About 3–6 ms a question over the full fixture once the patterns are built (warm; 12 ms in a cold worker with new terms).

Alone, 73 routes × (about 7.5 + 4.5 ms) = 0.8 s; on a core shared 16 ways, 14 s. That is the state's 13–16 s.

**The rule, as built** (test file only; the cache and `routeByQuestions` do not change): the cache keys on the text typed — not on its terms and not on what the body says (`a question differing only in case or spaces is a different key` already pins that) — so counting misses needs neither a new term nor a full body.

1. `typed(n)` is `graph embeddings protein structure cathode (${n})`: 72 distinct texts, one set of terms. `tokenize` drops a token shorter than three characters, so a number under 100 is no term. The questions are still routed (five specific terms, not vague).
2. `shortBody()` — the fixture's six sections with each paragraph cut to its first 100 characters (a new array each call, one entry per map row, index-aligned with `map`). "is per body" routes its **second** body (`elsewhere`) with it; "holds a bounded number" routes its one body with it. "Mine" in "is per body" stays the full real body. The map stays shared by the two bodies, so the test still tells "per body" from "per map".

No skip, retry, timeout or weaker assertion. The eviction count is unchanged: 72 misses on the second body (ROUTE_CACHE_LIMIT × 3), 25 on the bounded test's body (ROUTE_CACHE_LIMIT + 1).

Attribution of the gain (alone, 10 interleaved runs each, same machine state): original 855 ms / 396 ms (is per body / bounded); short body alone 496 / 231; shared terms alone (full body) 249 / 92; both **65 / 30**. Both levers are needed: either alone leaves 0.25 s or 0.5 s, which is 4 s or 8 s at the 16-way share.
