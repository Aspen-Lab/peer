## 0. The answer

- **A place that covers none of the card's words exists in the general case, but it is far from the line.** For the three-button group, a zero-word place inside the band exists for **875 of 925** cells (95%). Of the **511** cells where today's place covers words (55%), it exists for **461** (90%); the other **50** are all Explain-card cells in a window shorter than 900 px, where the card fills the band. The move it needs, for those 461: **median 163 px (x free) / 178 px (x fixed), 90th percentile 304 / 311, max 580**; **395 of 461 (86%) need more than 60 px** (406 with x fixed); only 66 are within 60 px.
- **Why it is far: a card has no gap the group fits in.** The group is 88.3 px tall. The tallest gap in an Explain card is 42 px (35 at 1024 and 1093), and none is 59 px. The card is as wide as the margin and the widest text-free column inside it is 13 px (the group is 109 wide). So a zero-word place is *outside* the card — above it (260 of the 461 places) — or in a blank part of a card that has one (the notes window's empty body, a sticky's empty body, the figure's picture box: 201 of 461). For Explain, which follows the selection and for a first-line selection has its top 5 px above the line, "above the card" is **93.3 px** (the group's 88.3 + 5) from the line, at best; for a selection on a later line of the paragraph it is 180 px, because the card starts higher.
- **Explain is the bulk of the extra, and the hardest part.** 330 of the 511 cells with words under the group are Explain cells (165 of 165 first-line selections, 165 of 165 fourth-line). **No Explain cell has a zero-word place within 60 px** (0 of 330). Where the other cards cover words (sticky 20 of 113 cells, figure card 33 of 165, stack of three 33 of 164, notes window 95 of 153), the cost is much smaller: sticky median 0 / p90 63 / max 83, figure and stack median 0 / p90 68 / max 130 (all the card's cells, x fixed).
- **The strip is easier**: a zero-word place exists for all 925 cells; of the 320 cells where today's strip covers words, the move is median 66 px / p90 128 / max 308 with x free (104 / 240 / 500 with x fixed); 163 of the 320 need more than 60 px (201 with x fixed).
- **Recommendation: (c), leave it.** No rule that stays within the walk's 60 px removes the extra: rule (a) with N = 60 moves 55 of 925 group cells (6%) and leaves **456 of 511** with words, and every Explain cell still has them. The one rule that does something for Explain (N = 100, the group above the card) clears **82 of 165** first-line cells, none of the fourth-line cells, and doubles the cells over 60 px (114 → 236). Taking a zero-word place wherever one exists (N = any) leaves 50 cells with words anyway and moves **459 of 925 cells over 60 px (median 58, p90 250, max 580)**. §6 has the three rules with their consequences; §7 names the functions a C would touch if the manager decides otherwise. With no card nothing changes under any of them (§5).

## 7. Recommendation, and what a C would touch

**(c).** The extra is mostly Explain's (330 of 511 cells): every selection beside an Explain card has words under the group, and for Explain nothing within 60 px is clear of words (0 of 330 cells), the nearest place being 93 px (first-line selections, above the card) or 180 px (later lines), or none (50 cells). Rule (a) with N = 60 is cheap (+1.8 px on average, never more than 60) but changes 6% of the group's cells and none of Explain's, so the readers' extra would be reported again unchanged; N = 100 is the first value that touches Explain and it clears half of the first-line cells at the cost of doubling the cells over 60 px and of a group standing 93 px away from the line it is for (B2: "level with the selection's first line"); N = any trades a cosmetic extra for 459 cells over 60 px and still leaves 50 cells with words. If the manager wants the strip's share of the extra gone, (b) with N = 60 for the strip alone is the best-priced rule (+3.4 px average, nothing over 60 px added, 320 → 163 cells with words), but it does not remove the extra either while the group stands over Explain's quote.

If a rule is wanted anyway (the functions, in order of cost):
