## 2. The rules as built

- **M8** (`joinProse`): the 10th percentile of x (`LEFT_EDGE_QUANTILE`, unchanged) is taken over the lines of each page (`leftOf`, a map page → edge) and a line is an indent when `line.x > leftOf(line.page) + 6` on the same page as the line above. Everything else in `joinProse` (the gap rules, the float rule, the page-break rule, blocks) is untouched. Block lines still count in the page's x list, as they did in the section's. A page with ten lines or fewer takes its minimum (as a short section did before).
- **M3 c3_last** (`closingPartition`): after the loop over the row's last `CLOSING.lastParagraphs` paragraphs finds no closing, in a paper that names no discussion or conclusion and for the same row and under the same guards as the loop (the whole function above the loop is unchanged, the echo check included), the row's **last paragraph** is the closing when the row has at least two paragraphs, it has at least `OUTLOOK_MIN_WORDS` = 60 words and it holds a cue of `OUTLOOK_CUES` — whatever it cites and however little it echoes. The split is the loop's own shape: results `0 … last-1`, conclusion `last … last`. `CLOSING` (exported, pinned by a test) is not touched; the floor is a constant of its own.
- **The cue list** (`OUTLOOK_CUES`, one list, six entries, case-insensitive). Phrases, not bare words, so a technical sense is no cue:
  1. `hold(s) [great|considerable|significant|substantial|real|much|enormous|strong] promise|potential` (not "holding potential", not "holds the potential");
  2. `pave(s|d)/paving the way`;
  3. a horizon opened or widened: `new|wider|broader|further|distant horizon(s)`, `on the horizon`, `expand|extend|widen|open|push (up) the|our|its|their horizon(s)` (not "soil horizon", not "time horizon");
  4. a scope to broaden: `broaden(s|ing) the|its|our|their|this|these`, or `to|could|can|will|may|might|would broaden` not followed by a preposition, a conjunction, an adverb of degree or a stop (not "line broadening", not "the peaks broaden with heat");
  5. `open(s|ing) (up) the way|door` and `open(s|ing) (up) new avenues|possibilities|opportunities|routes|directions`;
  6. `we envisage`.

## 7. For the manager

1. **Cue wording is narrower than B's prototype.** B's regex matched bare `horizon`, bare `broaden(s|ing)` and `we envisage|anticipate|expect`. A bare `horizon` is a soil horizon or a time horizon, `broadening` a line broadening, and `we expect` a results sentence; so the cues here are the phrases of §2. If the real P5 paragraph carries its outlook only in a bare form (a "horizon" or "broaden" not matched by entries 3 and 4), the fallback will not fire on it. **I could not check this** (no owner PDF read). The re-score on P5 settles it; the widening is one line (entries 3 and 4 of `OUTLOOK_CUES` in `reading-partition.ts`).
2. The fallback stays behind the function's existing guards, **the echo check included**: a paper with neither an abstract nor a setup part to take the echo from still gets no split (as B's prototype). "Whatever its echo" means the share of the echo, not its existence. P5 has an introduction, so it has an echo.
3. M8: a page of ten lines or fewer takes its minimum x as its edge. A short page that carries one margin label at a smaller x would read its other lines as indents; a section of that size had the same limit before. B measured 0 tests moved and the six maps (P1–P7); I did not re-measure on real papers.
4. `closingPartition` is also read by `structure-review.ts` and `app/papers/[id]/page.tsx`: both see the fallback. The full suite is green with it.
5. Overlap with C P12-75: none in code — mine is `joinProse` and `closingPartition` plus the three constants beside `FIGURE_REFERENCE`; the two edits to `pdf-outline.ts` (`joinProse`'s head and its indent test) are separate hunks from `buildOutline`.
