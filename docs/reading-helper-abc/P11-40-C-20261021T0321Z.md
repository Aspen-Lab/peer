## The defect, and the rule (ruling §1h.138 (c))

With a selection standing and its buttons up (no card open), Escape left the paper for the briefing (the legend's "Esc BACK": `reader-keys.ts` action `back`, which the keyboard layer calls for Escape and for Backspace). After Escape closed the front card, a second Escape with the selection still standing left too (`x32-escape-selection-*.json`; the walk's F15 line, X-5). The rule, as built: **Escape does one thing at a time, nearest first.**

1. A selection standing with its buttons (the page holds one: `explainTarget !== null`) is put away: the document's selection ends and the page's hold on it is let go, so the three buttons go. Nothing else: no card closes, the page stays.
2. Else the front card closes (`takeFrontEscape`, P11-17c, as it was: one Escape one card). The highlight's strip, which stands over a mark and takes its own Escape (`takeStripEscape`), went before the cards already and still does.
3. Else it is the page's `back` to the briefing, as the legend says.

Backspace is `back` still, whatever stands. An Escape typed into a field (the key's target, or the element that has the focus: input, textarea, select, an editable block) is the field's, as it was; a modal dialog over the page (`MODAL_OVER_PAGE`) and a key something has handled already (`defaultPrevented`) keep theirs. The table, the help sheet and the legend are untouched, so the legend's words are unchanged.

## Unsettled / for the manager

1. **A selection scrolled out of view still stands**: its buttons are not drawn (the pill is hidden), but `explainTarget` is non-null, so one Escape ends it unseen before the next one acts. I kept the rule on the page's state (what the buttons are drawn from) and did not add a visibility test; if A wants "nearest visible", it is one condition in the page's `standing` (`explainTarget.measure?.()` against the masthead's foot and the window's foot), no hook elsewhere.
2. **A selection the page does not hold** (text selected across two paragraphs, in a heading's neighbours, on a page with no body) has no buttons, so Escape does not end it: it goes on to the cards and then back, as before. That is the rule's words ("a selection standing with its buttons").
3. **The capture phase puts the selection before any Escape handler of a control that is not a field**: the left rail's peek (`reader-shell.tsx`, a document bubble listener) closes on the second Escape rather than the first when a selection also stands; a notes-window swatch row's own Escape would likewise (from the code; not walked). A field, a modal and a handled key are still theirs. No test or walk step fell on this.
4. A keyboard-made selection (shift + arrows) is settled after 150 ms (`SELECTION_DEBOUNCE_MS`); an Escape inside that wait is `back`. A mouse selection is settled at the button's release.
5. No hook was needed in `selection-toolbar.tsx`, `highlight-strip-host.tsx` or `explain-box.tsx` (C P11-38 and P11-39 are free of this change); the page's `back` could not tell Escape from Backspace, so the ladder stands in front of it rather than inside it (the brief's "the `back` action's handling"): the `back` closure is byte-identical.
6. The base's full-suite count (9920) is derived by subtraction from the red commit's run; I did not run the unmodified base suite.
