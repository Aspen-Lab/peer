# P10-05 — B: the model's partition of a paper with no headings (review 3.3, second half; rubric A15)

Role: B, the investigator. Read-only on production code; no model call, no network, no key. Head at the start: `35a2274f` (branch `deep-report-reading-helper-enhancement`), detached worktree. Two throw-away vitest files measured the extractor and the map on fixtures; they are not committed (the numbers below are what they printed). No sentence of any paper is in this note: the fixtures I built are invented ("a survey of shore ecology"), and the owner's PDF was not opened.

## 0. The answer in six lines

1. **Unheaded** means: the paper's largest `body`-bucket section holds at least half of the words, has at least eight paragraphs, and the rest of the paper has at most two sections with a placed bucket. The rule is one pure function over the sections the body already has (`id`, `canonical`, `paragraphs`), so the server and the browser compute it alike.
2. **The field** is a `partition` on the paragraph guide: `{ sectionId, parts: [{ role, fromParagraph, toParagraph }] }`, roles `introduction | results | discussion | conclusion | experimental`, over the paragraph indices of that one section (the "trunk"). The model is asked only for where each part starts; contiguity and coverage are made by the sanitizer, not hoped for.
3. **The request** is the paragraph-gist pass (`POST /api/papers/[id]/paragraph-guide`): it already sends every paragraph, once per document, and is already remembered by the document's hash alone. No new call, about 2–3 % more input and 5–8 % more output tokens on a 30-paragraph paper. The deep report cannot carry it (it reads sections, not paragraph indices); the explain route is per selection.
4. **The map** replaces the trunk's one row, in place, by group rows (level 1) named for the role, tagged "Peer's reading", each holding its slice of the paper's own paragraph lines (real indices, so anchors, openings and gists keep working). The body is not touched. Without a key the map is what it is today: one row, its paragraph lines (measured, never empty).
5. **The route** scores the paragraphs of the trunk (one grade, shared by the body and the map); a group row's mark is derived from its paragraphs (strongest paragraph tier, summed counts, best sentence), with at most half the groups strong.
6. **The C** is two Cs, medium each: C1 (pure module, prompt, sanitizer, route, hook) can start now; C2 (map overlay, route fold, page) must follow P10-04 and P10-06, which own the same files. The one choice for the manager is in section 3.4.

## 1. When is a paper "unheaded"?

### 1.1 What the extractors yield (measured)

`readableSections(doc)` drops the abstract and any section with no paragraphs; `roleOf(canonical)` is `body` for a bucket nothing recognised.

| input | what comes out | rows | measured on |
|---|---|---|---|
| PDF with no heading line at all | one section, `canonical: "body"`, heading = the paper's title when the title is set larger than the body (the title line is read as a heading before the title is skipped), else `"Body"` | 1 | `n2` (invented, 24 paragraphs, 1,248 words) |
| PDF, unheaded main text, then a "Methods" heading and "References" | the main text is one `body` row (heading = the title), "Methods" is a `methods` row, its bold sub-heads become further `body` rows, References end the argument | 4 | `n1` (23 + 2 + 2 + 2 paragraphs) |
| HTML (generic extractor) with no `<h2>`/`<h3>` | one section `Body`, canonical `body`, paragraphs split on the block ends | 1 | `html no-h2` (24 paragraphs) |
| HTML (generic) with one `<h2>` after ten paragraphs | the ten paragraphs before the first heading are dropped; only the section after it survives | 1 | `html one-h2` (a pre-existing extraction gap, not this item's; see section 7) |

So an unheaded paper is not a paper with zero rows; it is a paper whose text sits in one `body` row, in the PDF case with the paper's title as that row's heading. The abstract is not removed from it when the PDF has no "Abstract" heading, so the abstract paragraph is paragraph 0 of the trunk.

### 1.2 The measurements behind the rule

27 fixtures that exist (3 extracted-document JSONs in `lib/papers/__fixtures__`; 24 invented PDFs from the P9 and earlier scratchpads, read through `extractPdfTextFromPath`) plus 5 I built for this note (`minimalPdf` and the generic HTML extractor, invented text). For each: sections, rows with a placed bucket (`roleOf` not `body`, not `apparatus`), the largest `body` row's share of all words and its paragraphs.

| group | n | sections | placed rows | largest `body` row: share / paragraphs | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| headed PDFs and documents (every other fixture) | 25 | 3–15 | 3–9 | 0 % / 0 (24 of 25); 10 % / 1 paragraph (the one fixture with six unplaced sub-headings among fifteen rows) | headed |
| tiny PDFs with no heading (`p9c28/invented.pdf`, `p9b25/synth/vector.pdf`) | 2 | 1 | 0 | 100 % / 1 and 2 paragraphs, 11 and 122 words | too small to partition: stays one row |
| `n1` unheaded main text + Methods + References | 1 | 4 | 1 | 79 % / 23 | unheaded |
| `n3` the same without References | 1 | 4 | 1 | 79 % / 23 | unheaded |
| `n2` no heading at all | 1 | 1 | 0 | 100 % / 24 | unheaded |
| `html no-h2` | 1 | 1 | 0 | 100 % / 24 | unheaded |
| `html one-h2` | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 % / 0 | headed (by its one heading) |

(25 + 2 + 5 = 32 lines.) The owner's own paper, as screenshot 3.5 shows it, has 12 sections, all with a bucket: headed. Nothing measured sits between 10 % and 79 %, so the threshold is not fitted to a hair; it is set by reasoning about the target.

### 1.3 The rule

```ts
// reading-partition.ts (pure; no I/O, no model)
export const TRUNK = { share: 0.5, paragraphs: 8, placedRows: 2 } as const;

/** The section a model may partition, or null. `sections` is `readableSections(doc)` on the server and `reading.body` in the browser. */
export function unheadedTrunk(
  sections: ReadonlyArray<{ id: string; canonical: string; paragraphs: readonly string[] }>,
): { sectionId: string; sectionIndex: number; paragraphCount: number } | null
```

- words of a section = the sum of `paragraph.split(/\s+/).length` (the map's own count, `wordCount`);
- the **trunk** is the section with `canonical === "body"` that has the most words;
- **unheaded** if `trunk.words >= 0.5 * allWords`, `trunk.paragraphs >= 8`, and the sections whose canonical is not `body`, `abstract`, `references`, `acknowledgments` or `supplementary` number at most two;
- ties on words: the earlier section.

Why 0.5 and not the 0.7 the brief floated: a Nature-style article's Methods section is long (my estimate from the form, not a measurement: Methods are commonly 40–50 % of the words), so with the Methods heading recognised and the main text unheaded the trunk is near 55–60 %. 0.7 would pass `n1` (79 %, because my invented Methods are short) and fail the real thing. Nothing headed in the fixtures goes above 10 %, so 0.5 costs no false positive. Why eight paragraphs: fewer cannot make two parts of more than a paragraph or two; five labels over a page is noise, and the model is not worth calling for it. Why at most two placed rows: a paper with three recognised headings is mapped by its own headings (P10-04); the partition is for the text those headings do not cover.

What the rule leaves to P10-04: a paper whose body is split into several unplaced headings (no single `body` row holds half the words) is a paper with headings, mapped by them.

Confirmed: the rule is a function of the document, so it is the same for every reader and can be cached with the document.

## 2. Where the partition comes from with a key

### 2.1 Which request

| carrier | carries the paragraphs? | cached per paper, no reader in the key? | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| paragraph-gist pass (`/api/papers/[id]/paragraph-guide`) | yes: every paragraph with an opening, `{ sectionId, paragraphIndex, topicSentence, text ≤ 1,200 chars }`, up to 120, document order | yes: `paragraphGuideCache`, key = the document hash alone, one hour, in process; and the browser keeps the outcome a day per paper under `peer-paragraph-guide-v1` | **this one** |
| deep report (`/api/papers/report`) | no: Pass 1 reads sections (`bodySections`), Pass 2 reads a compressed signal; no paragraph index is on the wire | its caches are by document and questions, but its shape is the report | no |
| explain route | one passage and its neighbours | per selection | no |
| the shared reading (`/api/papers/[id]/reading`) | the whole body | yes, at the edge for a day | **no: it is built with no model and no user data (Tier 0 for everyone); a model's reading must not be put in it** |

So the partition is not in `PaperReading`; the map overlay is applied in the browser from the guide.

The gate is the gist pass's own: a provider that can write, and `providerConfigured && deepReportRequested` (the Deep report switch or an attached PDF), i.e. the paper's text already leaves on that one switch. No new datum leaves: the paragraphs sent are the same, so the privacy page's description of this pass stays true (the C re-reads the pinned paragraphs near line 205 of `app/privacy/page.test.tsx` and changes nothing unless it is false). The partition is asked for only when `unheadedTrunk(readableSections(doc))` is non-null, so a headed paper's prompt, answer and cost are byte-identical to today.

### 2.2 The field

```ts
export const PARTITION_ROLES = ["introduction", "results", "discussion", "conclusion", "experimental"] as const;
export type PartitionRole = (typeof PARTITION_ROLES)[number];

export interface GuidePart { role: PartitionRole; fromParagraph: number; toParagraph: number } // inclusive, indices of the trunk section
export interface GuidePartition { sectionId: string; parts: GuidePart[] }

export interface ParagraphGuide { docHash: string; gists: GistRecord; partition?: GuidePartition }
```

Invariants (all enforced by the sanitizer, none left to the model): at least two parts; the first starts at 0; each part starts one after the previous ends; the last ends at the section's last paragraph; every index is a whole number in range; no two neighbouring parts share a role; a role is used by at most two parts. Roles map to the existing `ReadingRole` for `gistRoute` and the row's role: introduction to `setup`, experimental to `method`, results to `evidence`, discussion and conclusion to `interpretation`.

### 2.3 The prompt lines

Added to the guide prompt only when a trunk exists. The model is asked for where each part **starts** (so a wrong boundary moves one edge instead of leaving a hole or an overlap); the stored shape still has `toParagraph`, derived.

System prompt: unchanged.

User prompt (JSON), additions:

- `task`: "Write one gist for every paragraph in `paragraphs`. Also divide the paragraphs of section `<sectionId>` into parts."
- `outputSchema.partition`: `[{ role: "one of: introduction, results, discussion, conclusion, experimental", fromParagraph: "the paragraphIndex of the part's first paragraph, a number" }]`, placed before `gists`.
- `rules`, appended:
  - "`partition` divides the paragraphs of section `<sectionId>` only, in reading order, into consecutive parts; list where each part starts; the first part starts at the section's first paragraph; a part runs until the next one starts. `paragraphIndex` values may skip: a part's range is by index."
  - "Roles: `introduction` is the background, motivation and the question asked; `results` is what was observed or measured; `discussion` is interpretation, comparison with other work and limits; `conclusion` is the closing summary and outlook; `experimental` is materials, samples, instruments, procedures, data processing and statistics, wherever in the section they stand."
  - "Use a role at most twice. Use only the roles the paragraphs show; leave a role out rather than invent a part. Two to five parts is usual. This is your reading of the structure: copy no sentence of the paper into it."

`gistMaxTokens(paragraphs, withPartition)` adds 300 when a partition is asked (about 5 parts at 18 tokens, and room).

### 2.4 The sanitizer and the verifier

`sanitizePartition(raw, trunk) -> GuidePartition | null`, pure, in `reading-partition.ts` (the same function normalises what the browser reads back from storage, so a corrupt entry cannot draw a broken map):

1. `raw.partition` must be an array; each entry an object with a `role` string (trimmed, lower-cased; `method` and `methods` are read as `experimental`; anything else is dropped) and a `fromParagraph` that is a whole number or a string of digits, `0 <= n < paragraphCount`.
2. Order kept: an entry whose start is not greater than the last kept start is dropped (no reordering, no duplicates).
3. Neighbouring entries with the same role are merged (the first start stands).
4. A role beyond its second part is dropped (its paragraphs fall to the part before it); then step 3 again.
5. The first part's start is set to 0.
6. Fewer than two parts: `null` (a single label says nothing).
7. `toParagraph` of each part is the next part's start minus one; the last part's is `paragraphCount - 1`.

There is **nothing to verify against the text**: the partition quotes nothing and asserts no fact of the paper; it is Peer's reading, labelled `PEERS_READING` on screen. The structural checks above are its whole verifier; the worst a wrong answer does is mislabel a range, visibly marked as Peer's. (The gists keep their grounding rule, unchanged.)

Route behaviour changes the C must make: today `kept === 0` returns `{ unavailable: true }`; with a partition the guide is still worth keeping (`gists: {}` with a partition), so the test becomes `kept === 0 && !partition`. The browser's `cleanGists` returns null when empty and `readOutcome` then rejects the whole entry; it must accept a guide that has a partition and no gists. A guide with neither is `unavailable`, as now.

### 2.5 The cost (confirmed from the code, no call made)

`buildParagraphGuidePrompt` already puts every candidate paragraph in the prompt, each as `text` clipped to 1,200 characters (about 300 tokens at the cap), plus its `topicSentence`. For a 30-paragraph trunk with a Methods section behind it (about 35 candidates) that is up to about 10,000 input tokens and about 1,600 answer tokens (`45 * paragraphs + 200`). The partition adds about 220 input tokens (the rules and the schema line) and about 90–120 output tokens: roughly 2–3 % more in, 5–8 % more out, **no second call and no per-visit call** (the guide is asked once per paper per browser-day and once per document-hour per server process). The existing cap stays: more than 120 candidate paragraphs and the pass is skipped, so no partition either (an honest limit; the map is then Tier 0's one row).

### 2.6 Caching, and the versions

- Server: `paragraphGuideCache` entry gains `partition`; key unchanged (document hash), no reader. A server restart clears it.
- Browser: `peer-paragraph-guide-v1` becomes `-v2`. The old entries are valid gists, but without a partition they would keep an unheaded paper at one row for up to a day; the bump makes it ask once.
- `PaperReading.version`: **no bump for this item** (nothing is added to the reading). C P10-04 bumps it for `level` / `parentId`.
- Report cache (`peer-paper-report-cache-v4`, `paperReportCacheKey`) and `UPLOAD_CONCEPT_EXTRACTION_VERSION`: no change; the report's shape does not change.

## 3. What the map shows

### 3.1 With the partition

The overlay `partitionedMap(map, body, partition)` (pure, new module) replaces the trunk's row, **in the same position**, by one row per part. Nothing is reordered; rows before and after the trunk keep their order.

```
Read from PDF · 1 section · 1,248 words · about 7 min      (the paper's own sections; see 3.3)
Peer's reading — not a quote
[+] Introduction                         Peer's reading · 410 words · 3 min
[+] Results                              Peer's reading · 640 words · 4 min
[+] Discussion                           Peer's reading · 198 words · 2 min
[+] Methods (a real heading in the PDF)  experimental · p.4 · 118 words · 1 min     <- the paper's own row, unchanged
```

A group row is an ordinary `ReadingMapSection` plus a small `part` record:

| field | value |
|---|---|
| `id` | `<sectionId>~<n>` (`s0~0`, `s0~1`, ...), unique, stable for one partition |
| `heading` | the role's label ("Introduction", "Results", "Discussion", "Conclusion", "Experimental"), a `MAP.parts` copy string |
| `canonical` | the role id; `role` the mapped `ReadingRole` (so `gistRoute` orders groups by it) |
| `level` | 1 (P10-04's field; `parentId` absent) |
| `paragraphs` | the trunk row's lines for `fromParagraph..toParagraph`, **with their real indices and openings** |
| `words`, `minutes` | recomputed from the slice with the map's own counter; the totals over the map are unchanged |
| `page` | absent (the trunk's page is the first part's, not the others') |
| `part` | `{ sectionId, sectionIndex, role, from, to }` |

The row's meta reads "Peer's reading" where a headed row reads its role, then words and minutes. The legend line (`PEERS_READING`, now shown only when a gist renders) is also shown when any group row is on screen. The row's link goes to `#paragraphAnchor(sectionIndex, from)`; the paragraph lines go to `#paragraphAnchor(sectionIndex, line.index)`; gists are looked up under the trunk's `sectionId`, not the group id. All three need the `part` record because today the view derives the anchor from the row's position `k` and the gist key from `row.id`; a replaced row changes both.

Two traps for the C:
- `ReadingMapView` keeps its open rows as a `Set<number>` of positions. The partition arrives after the first paint, replacing one row by several and shifting every row after it; the open set must be keyed by row id.
- `route.byQuestion[].sections` is a record keyed by row id and the body reads only real ids, so group marks are extra keys beside the real ones; the body is unaffected.

### 3.2 Without a key (confirmed, measured)

The map is what it is today: for `n2` and `html no-h2` one row (heading = the title or "Body", no role tag), 24 paragraph lines, 1,248 words; for `n1` four rows. Never empty: `ReadingMapView` returns null only when there is no body at all. The row is folded by default ("+"), so the reader sees a single closed row. Optional, for the manager: open a single-row map by default (`openRows` for `map.sections.length === 1`), which is a two-line change in the view but in P10-04's region; I would do it in C2 if P10-04 has landed. Not required by A15 ("never empty, never broken").

Tier 0 stays complete without a key (§1b): nothing in the unheaded rule, the overlay or the route fold needs the model; only the labels need it.

### 3.3 The summary line

`MAP.summary(sections, ...)` is given the paper's own section count (the unpartitioned map's), not the number of rows on screen, so a PDF with no headings says "1 section", not "4 sections" that the paper does not have. Words and minutes are the same either way.

## 4. The route and the tint (P10-06's score over sections)

The unit scored is the **paragraph**, inside the real trunk section; a group row's mark is **derived** from its paragraphs. The group is not scored as a section.

Why not the group as a section: the partition has three to five groups. P10-06's rule is `idf = ln((N+1)/(df+1)) + 0.1` over N sections, "strong = score at least 0.6 of the maximum and among the top three": with N = 4 a term in two groups weighs nearly the same as a term in one, and three of four rows may be strong, which is the owner's complaint (3.4) in miniature. Scoring groups would also give the body and the map two different grades for the same paragraph (the body reads the trunk's id), so a line would be tinted differently in the two places.

What is done (a pure fold, `withPartitionMarks(route, map, body, partition)`, run after `readingRoute` and `mergeQuestionRoute`, with the memo keyed on the route and the partition):

- the trunk row keeps its P10-06 route entry (its paragraphs graded: the top paragraphs strong, the rest faint, the rest unmarked);
- each group id gets an entry in every question's `sections`: `tier` = the strongest tier among its paragraphs, `paragraphs` = the trunk's graded paragraphs that fall in its range (the map's lines, same grades as the body), `hits` = the question terms counted over the group's paragraphs only (facts a reader can check, the mentions line), `evidence` = the best matching sentence in the group, verbatim;
- at most `max(1, floor(N / 2))` groups are strong (best score first, document order on a tie); the rest are shown at the next tier. This is the small-N guard: with five groups at most two, with one row (no partition) the single row is strong as P10-06 says;
- the gist question ("Just get the gist") takes each group's tier from its role through the existing `GIST_TIER`.

What keeps **A9** true (graded, three greens ordered by relevance): groups carry the same tiers as any row, derived from paragraphs that carry them. What keeps **A11** true (selective: at most three strong, at least a third at none or faint): strong paragraphs are capped at three by P10-06's rule 3, a group is strong only if it holds one, and the half-cap holds it to at most two of five groups; a question whose terms are in one part leaves the other parts unmarked. The C proves it on the invented fixture (a term in one paragraph: exactly one group strong, the others none; a term in every part: at most `floor(N/2)` strong).

Unsettled for P10-06's C or the manager: the paragraph grade (P10-06 rule 3) is what makes this work. If rule 3 slips, the fold falls back to "tier = best section tier" and every group inherits the trunk's tier, which is the old behaviour.

- **The abstract sits in the trunk** on a PDF with no "Abstract" heading (paragraph 0), so the first part contains it. The roles have no "abstract"; it rides in the introduction. Not a defect of this item; noted so a reviewer is not surprised.
- **The PDF title becomes the trunk row's heading** (measured), so a Tier 0 reader sees one row named for the paper's title. After the partition it is replaced by the parts; the body keeps the title as its first section heading, as today.
- **Sub-heads inside Methods** become separate `body` rows (measured on `n1`: two further `body` rows after "Methods"): they are not part of the trunk and the partition does not touch them. Whether they inherit "methods" is P10-04's hierarchy.
- **The HTML generic extractor drops the text before the first `<h2>`/`<h3>`** (measured on `html one-h2`: ten of fourteen paragraphs gone). Real publisher pages usually have their own extractor and headings, but a page with a lead of unheaded paragraphs before its first heading loses them. Pre-existing and outside this item; I did not raise a task (instructed not to call the session task tools). Worth a line in the ledger.
- **More than 120 candidate paragraphs** skips the whole pass, partition included. A long unheaded paper then stays at one row. A cheaper partition-only request (topic sentences only) would cover it; not proposed here.
- **A model-written "Read next" mark on the trunk** (`mergeQuestionRoute`'s model entries, keyed by section id) applies to the trunk id, not to the group rows; the groups do not inherit it. Acceptable; say so in the C2 checkpoint.
- The model is never run here, so the prompt wording in 2.3 is untested against a model; the C1 gate is the stubbed route, and the first real answer is read in the owner's walk. The sanitizer is written so a poor answer degrades to one row, never to a broken map.
