## The rules as built

**R6a (B P12-39, M6).** A new pattern `CAPTION_SEPARATED` in `pdf-outline.ts` (next to `CAPTION`), used by `captionOf`. It has `CAPTION`'s three groups (label, number, words) and takes the words exactly as `CAPTION` did. After the label's number it needs, by a look-ahead that consumes nothing: an optional space and then `.` `:` `|` or a dash (the hyphen, U+2010 to U+2015, the minus sign U+2212; the en and em dash are inside that range); or a space and a capital letter; or the end of the line (a label standing alone, its words on the next line). `)` `,` `;` after the number refuse, and so does a space and a small letter ("Figure 3 shows ..."), a space and a bracket ("Fig. 5 (left) ..."), and a letter or bracket joined to the number ("Fig. 1(a) ...", "Fig. 8a) ..."). The capital test cannot use the `i` flag (it would read a small letter as a capital), so the pattern is built with a six-line helper `anyCase` for the four label words and the roman numerals, without the flag, and with the `u` flag for `\p{Lu}`.

**R7a (B P12-39, M7).** In `continuesCaption`, the face test `next.font !== previous.font` now ends the caption unless the new helper `titleFaceChange(previous, next, gap, pitch, body)` lets it by. It does so when all of these hold: `previous` is the caption's first line (it satisfies `captionOf`; a later line never does, because `continuesCaption` already ends the caption at a line that does); the paper's line pitch is known (`pitch > 0`); and the gap between the two lines is one line pitch for the caption's size, `pitch * next.size / body.size`, within 20% (`TITLE_PITCH_TOLERANCE = 0.2`, so 0.8 to 1.2 pitches for a caption set at the text size). The size test (within 0.6 pt of the first line), the 1.5-pitch bound, the furniture, new-caption and numbered-heading tests that follow are unchanged. A second change of face goes through the old test (the previous line is no longer the caption's first line) and ends the caption. No caller changed: the first line is recognised by `captionOf(previous)`, so `buildOutline`'s loop is untouched. The `void body` line and its comment went, because `body` is now read.

## Choices the brief left to me

- `CAPTION` is left exactly as it was. `headingOf` calls `CAPTION.test(text)` to keep a line that opens with a figure's label out of the headings. Had I made `CAPTION` itself strict, a body line "Fig. 8) ..." set in another face or larger than the text would have become a heading candidate, which is a change to heading code the brief rules out. So the strict pattern is a separate constant and `captionOf` (one token) uses it; `headingOf` is byte-identical. A test (`keeps a line that opens with a figure's label out of the headings, caption or not`) pins that.
- No panel letter is accepted: the old pattern never matched "Fig. 8a" (no word boundary after the digit), and the brief's "optional panel letter" is read as "a panel letter, if one were there, would also need the separator". Adding panel-letter captions would be a new feature.
- The words of a caption are cut as before, so "Table 3 | Parameters" gives the words "| Parameters" as it did.
- The pitch the brief calls "the caption's" cannot be measured from a one-line caption, so the paper's pitch (the `pitch` argument) is used, scaled by the caption's size over the text size so that a caption set smaller than the text, with a tighter leading, is matched at its own leading.

- Shapes the old pattern took as a caption and the new one does not: a number followed by `)` `,` `;`, by a small letter after a space ("Figure 3 shows ..."), by a bracket ("Fig. 5 (left) ...", "Figure 1 (a) ..."), or joined to the number ("Fig. 1(a) ..."). - R7a cannot tell a bold title line over its text-face continuation from a one-line caption directly followed by prose at the same size, in the text face, one pitch below. Both look the same on the five facts the line carries; the second would now be absorbed. It is limited to a gap within 0.8 to 1.2 pitches (a figure is normally followed by more space than that), and the tolerance (0.2) and the size scaling are my choices, easy to tighten or drop.
