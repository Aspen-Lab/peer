## The rules as built

1. **A row that holds a drop cap is prose, never a heading.** `capRowOf` now needs only the cap's characters: the row's largest runs (within 0.9 of the tallest) hold one or two characters and every other run is set under 1/1.8 of them. It no longer requires that the cap open the row by x order, nor that no run of the row be marked raised or lowered, nor an exact baseline; the cap's letters and where its words start (`PdfLine.cap.letters`, `.textX`) are kept. `isDropCap` (unchanged) is then true for the row, so `headingOf` says nothing, and `openingStart` counts it (rule 3, as in item 7).
2. **The capital moves to the start of the paragraph's first line.** `placeDropCaps(lines, body)`, called in `buildOutline` once the body is known, finds for each drop-cap row the lines it stands beside: contiguous above the row (within 1.6 sizes of the words), on its page, in its face, at the size of its words, starting where its words start (`textX`, within one size), and within the cap's reach (0.75 of the cap's size less the words', plus 0.7 size). The capital is put on the first of them, joined to its first word when that word continues in lower case or in capitals ("S" + "nail" gives "Snail"), else with a space; the row it shares a baseline with loses the letters (and whatever `lineOf` had welded) and is an ordinary line of body size; the lines beside the cap start where the cap does (`joinProse` opens a paragraph at an indent, so each indented line had been a paragraph). Where there are no such lines nothing moves (the usual cap on a paragraph's first row; item 7 joins it in `lineOf`).
3. **The opening run counts these rows:** unchanged from item 7 (`bodySized`), and it also holds after the move because the rows are then ordinary.
