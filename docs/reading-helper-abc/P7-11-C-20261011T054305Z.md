## Judged differently from the brief (each small; none hidden)

1. **The brief's two examples for rule 2 are not red tests.** `⟨003⟩||ND texture was observed in the films` has two symbols (the two pipes; `⟨ ⟩` are not in `MATH_SYMBOL`), under the three-symbol floor, so it was never an equation; `E = mc^2` has two symbols too (`=`, `^`) and was never an *un*numbered display equation. The red test uses `⟨003⟩||ND + ⟨101⟩||ND texture was observed in the films` (five symbols, three words) and `x_i = y + z_j for each cell term`; the "stays" cases are `E = mc^2 (1)` (numbered), `softmax(QK^T/√d_k)V`, and the two-word line.
2. **A word may be separated by a hyphen as well as a space.** Taken literally (spaces only) the printout lands on 15 "equations", not the 14 the manager accepted; the one line that differs is "⟨003⟩||ND and ⟨110⟩||ND, ex-situ, indicate localized", whose third word is the "situ" of "ex-situ". With hyphen-separated parts counted the printout is 14 and the paper 21 (both definitions give 21 there).
3. **Rule 6's "ends in a letter (`[A-Za-z]{2,}$`)" overlaps its own "kept after a one- or two-letter unit token (`cm2`, `s−1`)".** `cm` is two letters. Built as: three letters or more → drop, one or two → keep, so `cm2`, `mm2`, `Li`+digit stay glued (the 35 exponents on the paper); the cost is the one citation after "Li".
4. **A citation mark must open with a digit, and consecutive raised runs are judged as one mark.** The paper stores "21−23" as three raised runs ("21", "−", "23 "). The brief's regex would drop all three one by one (each matches `[\d\s,–−-]+`), but the same regex also drops `mol` + `−` + `1`, losing an exponent's sign and digit (a citation never opens with a minus). Requiring a leading digit fixes that, and then a lone "−" run could be left behind ("prevention.−23") unless the runs are read together; so a cluster is judged as one string ("21−23" drops whole, "−1" after `mol` stays). This only ever *keeps* more than the brief's regex, never drops more.
5. **A raised script is a superscript when more than 1 pt above the row's baseline**, decided where the script is folded in `rowsOf` (a `Row.raised` set), and dropped in `lineOf`, once per column, so a citation at the head of a right-column line cannot take its context from the left column.
6. **The sentence test in `headingOf` uses the plausible section number too** (a decimal-led line ending in `.` is a sentence); tested.
7. **Added tests the brief did not list:** the real-PDF citation test in the columns file, the version pin, the exponent-fold sibling, the 9 / 10 / 11 line boundary for rule 3, the `Published data suggest …` pair for rule 4.
8. **One P7-10 assertion rewritten** (above).
