## 2. The rules as built

**How a card's controls are read (rule 1: "say how you read them").** `openCards` asks each open card, once, `CONTROL_SELECTOR`:
`button, [role=button], [role=link], [role=menuitem], [role=tab], [role=switch], [role=checkbox], [role=radio], [role=slider], [role=combobox], [role=toolbar], a[href], input, select, textarea, summary` — what an element *is*; no attribute the app made up (a test pins that the selector holds no `data-`, no class, no id). Then, for each element found:
1. it must be **drawn**: a form field always; else it has text, or an `svg` icon, or paints a surface of its own (a background colour or image, a border — from the computed style); a button that is only a hit area (the click under a note's last block, the veil behind a menu) and the figure card's picture button (it holds a `canvas` and paints nothing: the picture is the card's content) are no control; an element with `visibility: hidden` is none;
2. it is read at its **line boxes** (`getClientRects`: a link that wraps is two), **seen through** the card and every scrolling body between it and the card (computed overflow): a control scrolled out of sight is not under the group;
3. a control **inside one of the card's header rows** is left to the row (its `parts`: the X, the title's words, Open as page, Export, the zoom buttons): the raise button's empty width is the row's free part, as P11-38 built it;
4. a `[role=toolbar]` is a control as a whole row (the notes window's formatting row), and its buttons are too; a writing surface (`[contenteditable]`, `[role=textbox]`: a sticky's text, a note's blocks) is **not** a control: its words are the card's words.

The card's **words** are read the same way (`wordsOf`): every text node as the line boxes it stands in, seen through the card and its scrolling bodies, a subtree wholly out of sight not walked.

**The place (`clearOfHeaders`, rules 1–3).** A box is a *clear place* when it covers no control's box and meets no header row except wholly inside it, clear of what the row's text and controls hold (4 px). From the line's top the places are tried nearest first, the one below winning a tie: the line's own top; just above and just below every header and every control; and the nearest top wholly inside a row the box fits. At each top every left the margin allows is tried — the margin's ends, where it was, and the sides of whatever stands in reach (a control touching it or 4 px from it, what a header holds, a line of words) — and among the clear ones the left that covers least of the card's words wins; the margin's left edge is kept unless another saves more than about half a word (400 px² of line boxes; without that the group wandered along the margin as lines passed under it). Nowhere clear: held, as before. `how` is `level` (unmoved), **`aside`** (new: level with the line, slid along the margin), `free` (whole inside a header row), `below`, `above`, `held`; `data-header-clear` carries it.

1. **No control is covered.** The group and the strip never cover a control: the header row's, the notes window's formatting row and its buttons, a sticky's "Go to the passage" square, the explain card's link, input and Send. 0 of 778 selections (§6).
2. **The free part is a place only if the group fits.** An 88-px group does not fit a 40-px header (nor the explain card's 53-px row): it stands just above, just below (below the last of a stack) or level over the body where that covers no control. The 34-px strip does fit a 40-px row, and stands inside it as near the mark as the row allows. Smallest move first; over the body the fewest words.
3. **Truly nearest.** No clear place is nearer than the one taken, over invented scenes (a property test with a brute-force oracle written without the code's helpers: 400 scenes) and over the page (§6: 0 px of excess, 778 selections, by the rules' own definition of a clear place). Where r2's yardstick still shows an excess, §7 says why the nearer place is not clear.
4. **Nothing else changes.** Where no card stands at the line's height the place is `{ left: margin + 8, top: the line's, how: "level" }`, byte for byte (guards, `Object.keys` pinned); B2's floor, a window under 1024 px (the pill) and P11-38 rule 1 (the group put away after Explain) are guarded green, and a flow test presses Explain with controls on the page.

## 8. Unsettled — for the manager

- **The longer moves are the price of rules 1 and 2.** Explain: 0 → 48 px at every height (below the row), **99.5 at 1024** (the quote's link stands mid-width in a 280-px margin, so no left clears it: under the link, or 93 px above the row); notes window: 14 → 54 px (under the formatting row), 90 at 1024 and 1093; the stack of three unchanged (207). A reader who costs a long move will cost these; the rules say nearest clear place, and this is it.
- **The Explain card follows every new selection, so the group is always at its header**, and below the row it covers the quote's words: 4.3 on average before, 4.3 after (max 9 → 6, 8 fewer selections covering any). Above the row it would cover none, at about 93 px (+45). I took the brief's order — smallest move first, then the fewest words — and did not trade a move for words; a rule ("above, when it is within N px of below") is the manager's.
- **The figure's picture and the notes window's empty click area are not controls here**: both are `button`s the person can press anywhere, but they paint nothing of their own (the picture is the card's content). The group stands over the picture in 110 of 467 selections and over "Write at the end" in 43, as before. If the readers count the picture's button as a control, the group could never stand over a figure at all (the nearest clear place would be above or below the whole card, 300 px or more). Counts by `hitAreas` in the dumps.
- **Over a card's text the group sits at the right of the margin** (the left that covers fewest words, past 400 px² of saving), and at the margin's left edge elsewhere; the notes window's block handles (two 14×20 buttons at each block's left) slide it right by about 100 px. Both are by the rules.
- **A card's content is read at the next measure** (a scroll, a resize, a change of the stack), as the headers were: an Explain answer that arrives while a selection's group stands is not seen until then. A mutation observer on the cards was left out (the flow suite counts observers).
- Reading cost: see §6 (0.2 → 2.3 ms per measure in the heaviest stack). One cheap cut if wanted: reuse the cards' reading for a scroll whose target is not inside a card.
- The strip carries no data attribute (`highlight-strip.tsx` is not mine), so its `clear` is not on the page; its rects and the pictures are the evidence. Only 13 of the readers' 15 heights; the owner's PDF was not used.
