## 2. The cause, found by execution (the walk's reading was a lead, and it is wrong)

## 3. The rule as built

In `readSelection`, when the end is rolled back to the start (the browser ended the selection at offset 0 of another block, and the words carry no break inside them; the test is as it was), the selection's contents for the formula check and the words are read from **the range up to the end of the block the start is in** (`ownContents`: `range.cloneRange()`, `setEnd(block, block.childNodes.length)`, `cloneContents()`; `block` is the place's `text` element, the paper's `<p>`, a heading or a caption), not from the range as the browser ended it. The paragraph's own words and any formula drawn inside it are in that part; nothing of the next block is, whatever it is: a paragraph, a heading, a caption, a displayed equation's `code` or `.katex`, a figure, a rule. Where it cannot be had (a Summary or answer place, which has no `text` element; a stand-in with no `childNodes` or no `setEnd`; a throw) the range's own contents are used, exactly as before. The selection is the paragraph's, one block, offered Take notes and Highlight, and Explain this where the page offers it; the passage is the paragraph's words with a note tag's text left out (P10-24), as before. `measure`, the rects, the offset hint and the target are untouched. 34 lines added and 5 removed in `paper-body.tsx`, comments included (one optional member on `ElementLike`, `childNodes`; one function; the `endsAtNextStart` block).

One TypeScript detail worth knowing: the roll-back flag is a `let`, set inside the `if`. As a `const` computed from `!samePlace(start, end) && …` TypeScript narrows `start` to `never` where the flag is read (aliased conditions); `tsc` said so, and I changed it before the commit.

I did not reproduce it (the stub's Summary has no formula at a block's start). If a walk finds it, the place would need the block's element (the one `placeOf` already finds as a local) to clip to.
- **Chromium only.** Where Firefox or Safari end a triple click is not measured; a browser that ends it at the end of the paragraph's own `<p>` is the same place and never took the roll-back.
- **Not the owner's paper.** The invented PDF has PDF-style numbered equations (the `code` case the walk measured) and the KaTeX case was made by swapping the markup in the real page, not by an HTML-sourced paper.
- `Equation`'s markup is unchanged and was not the cause; `math.tsx` needed no change.
