### `question-examples.ts`

`phrasesFirst(text, template)` now takes the source's template and filters what `phrasesFromText(text)` gives (default `max` 8, as in P1-09b).

1. **A multi-word phrase is offered only when `isPhrase` holds.**
   - It has 2 to 6 words.
   - None of its words is in `SENTENCE_WORDS`: `we i our you your they it its this that these those he she is are was were be will can do does` (23 words).
   - Words are compared lower-cased, with leading and trailing punctuation stripped. The comparison is by whole word, so "Wearable", "itinerant" and "dopants" are fine.
   - A rejected phrase is skipped, the next one is tried, and it does not use a slot of the source's cap.
2. **The single-word fallback is used only when no phrase was accepted.**
   - The word is the first single-word item `phrasesFromText` gives.
   - It is returned only when `specificTerms(template(word)).length > 0`, using `specificTerms` from `reading-map.ts` on the finished example. A stoplist word ("study", "work", "results") or a generic word ("energy", "materials") yields nothing.
   - A source with nothing acceptable contributes no example.
3. **Topics and methods are unchanged**, as typed.

The header comment has a P1-09c paragraph. The cap logic (`MAX_PER_SOURCE`, `asked`, `offer`) is untouched.

## Observations for the manager (not changed)

1. **The fallback can still be an odd keyword.** The ruling asks only that the fallback word route on a specific term. "We test sulfide electrolytes" alone is rejected as a fragment, and its first keyword "test" is neither stoplisted nor generic. So the example is "How does this relate to test?" (measured). It routes on `test` and nothing else. Choosing a better fallback, for example the first keyword that is not a verb of the rejected sentence, is beyond the ruling.
2. **A function-word chunk is still a phrase.** The ruling's list has no `and`, `why` or `how`, and the specific-term check applies only to the fallback. "We study how dendrites nucleate under fast charging, and why" yields the chunk "and why", so the example is "How does this relate to and why?" (measured). A route-term check on multi-word phrases too (at least one specific term) would remove it; I did not add it.
3. **A fallback keyword can keep a sentence-final period.** `phrasesFromText` keeps `.` inside a keyword token ("superalloys.", "electrolytes."). The fallback takes the first single-word item, which is the chunk (period-free) whenever one exists. It could carry a period only if every chunk was longer than 10 words and the first keyword ended a sentence. I did not see it on any fixture and did not change it.
