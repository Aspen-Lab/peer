## K1. The card's caption is cut (P3 Fig. 1 at 135 characters, Fig. 6 at 63)

### The two caption sources, and which the card reads

1. The outline's caption. `buildOutline` reads the line stream: `captionAt` opens a caption (`pdf-outline.ts:3030`), `continuesCaption` extends it (`:3042`), the lines are folded and stored (`:3047-3048`, `figureCaptions`). `normalize` copies it into the document (`pdf-text.ts:239-251`, built in `normalize` at `:218`). `placeFigures` puts it on the figure (`reading.ts:698`, caption copied at `:746`, called at `:810`). The body's inline caption renders that same string (`paper-body.tsx:862`, `CaptionText`), and a click on the picture passes it to the card (`paper-body.tsx:935`, `openFigure`), which shows it (`figure-card.tsx:386`, the scrolling block at `:290-299`). So the inline caption in the body and the card text are one string, `ReadingFigure.caption`. The card reads source 1.
2. The engine's caption. `bestCaptionForRegion` (`pdf-engine.ts:410`) and `mergeCaptionLines` (`:377`: lines within 28 pt, ends at a 1.4x pitch jump, at most 1,500 characters) read MuPDF's lines and ignore faces. `pdf-extract.ts:237-248` puts it in the figure pool, `figure-binding.ts:400` copies it to the report, `report-sections.tsx:152/239/353` (`MattedFigure`) shows it as the Summary thumbnail's caption. That is why the same Fig. 1 shows 1,174 characters there. The engine's lengths for P3 are 1,174 / 1,201 / 979 / 1,500 / 966 / 544; the outline's cut ones (135, 63) are the only two that disagree by more than a label's length.

### Why the inline caption ends early on exactly Fig. 1 and Fig. 6

Instrumenting `buildOutline` (every caption's last line, the next line, and the first test of `continuesCaption` that fails) gives, for P3:

| Figure | lines taken after the opener | the test that ended it |
|---|---|---|
| Fig. 1 | 0 | face: opener face `g_d0_f8`, next line `g_d0_f3`, same size 8, gap 10.5 |
| Fig. 2 to 5 | 7, 6, 10, 6 | gap above 1.5 pitch (the prose resumes) |
| Fig. 6 | 0 | face: same two faces, same size, gap 10.5 |

Not R4 (`:398`; the next line is not numbered), not R6b or `endsParagraph` (`:1458-1500`; those only decide whether a line opens a caption), not a column change (the next line is below, gap +10.5) and not a page break. The rule is the face test, `pdf-outline.ts:390`:

`if (next.font !== previous.font && !titleFaceChange(previous, next, gap, pitch, body)) return false;`

Two facts make it fire:

- A line's face is the face of its longest run (`font: longestRun(runs).fontName`, `pdf-outline.ts:898`; `longestRun` at `:673`). A caption is a bold title run followed by regular text on the same first line. On Fig. 1 the bold run is 85 characters against 45 regular; on Fig. 6, 44 against 19; so the opener's face is the bold one and the next line (all regular) is "another face". On Fig. 2 to 5 the regular tail is the longer run, the opener's face is the regular face, and nothing changes face. That is a coin toss on the first line's proportions, not a property of the caption.
- The rescue, R7a `titleFaceChange` (`:349-359`), measures the gap against `pitch * next.size / body.size` (`:357`). `pitch` is `linePitch(read)` (`:324-334`), the 25th percentile of every same-page gap, figure-text rows included: 8.70 on P3 against a real leading of 10.5. So the expected leading is 8.70 x 8/9.3 = 7.48, the gap is 10.5, the tolerance is 0.2 x 7.48 = 1.50, and |10.5 - 7.48| = 3.02 fails. (With a correct pitch, 10.5 x 8/9.3 = 9.03, |1.47| passes by 0.34.)

The unconsumed lines then fall through to the body: after any of the fixes below P3's body is 1,508 characters shorter (they were a paragraph beside the figure labels, A72's O6).

## K2. Acronym case damage

### Cause: not in Peer's code

No step of Peer changes the case. The lowercase letter is already in pdf.js's text layer:

- Raw items (`readPages`, `pdf-text.ts:159-190`, items pushed unchanged at `:177-178`) on the P3 caption rows are short items of one to three letters, each its own item, in the caption's bold face (`g_d0_f8` in `readPages`' numbering: the one font whose mismatched capitals are exactly T, U, I, L and S, Whitney-Semibold), touching the item after (or before) it: a lowercase `s` then an item that starts `E`, a lowercase `l` then an item that starts `CO`, `DF` then a lowercase `t`, two lowercase `ss` then an item that starts `Bs`, a lowercase `i` then an item that continues in lowercase, a lowercase `l` then `i-`. The widths agree with capitals (a one-letter item 4.26 pt wide at 8 pt).
- `lineOf` (`pdf-outline.ts:741-901`) only appends the item text (`:871`); nothing there, in `joinProse`, in the heading code, in `cleanPaperText` or in `restoreLigatures` calls `toLowerCase` on text (the only lower-casing in `pdf-outline.ts` is for word lookups, `:1227`, `:1672`, `:1972`). The stage-by-stage trace shows the first damaged text at `linesOfPageWith`, i.e. already at item level.
- The PDF itself is the cause. The three Whitney fonts (Medium, Semibold, Bold) carry a `ToUnicode` map written with wrong array entries. Semibold's (object 547; encoding object 545 is WinAnsi plus two differences) holds `<49><50> [<0069><004A><004B><006C>...]` and `<52><56> [<0052><0073><0074><0075><0056>]`: the codes of capital I, L, S, T and U (0x49, 0x4C, 0x53, 0x54, 0x55) are mapped to the lowercase letters. pdf.js follows the map. MuPDF (the figure engine) and `pdftotext` read the encoding and give capitals, which is why the engine's captions (`Fig. 4 | Inter...`, `LCO`) are right and the outline's are not.

### Reach

A scan of every glyph operation in every PDF (code against Unicode, case-only mismatches on ASCII letter codes): P1 0 of 39,660 glyphs, P2 0 of 45,672, P3 224 of 66,052 (Medium 77: R, T, I, C, L, E, S; Semibold 77: T, U, I, L, S; Bold 70: U, R, E, L, S), P4 0 of 65,785, P5 0 of 66,872, P6 0 of 68,286. So only P3 is touched. The running-head word and the page-top labels carry most of the Medium and Bold ones and are dropped as furniture.

Visible in P3's parsed document (compared token by token with `pdftotext`): 10 occurrences in 5 of 6 captions and one section heading: `sE` x3 (Fig. 1, Fig. 3, the heading and with it the map row), `lCO` x2 (Fig. 2, Fig. 6), `lYC` (Fig. 6), `DFt` (Fig. 6), `ssBs` (Fig. 3), `li-` (Fig. 4), and Fig. 4's first letter (a capital I read as `i`). A72 counted four captions; Fig. 3 is a fifth (`sE ssBs`); only Fig. 5 is clean.
