## 2. The rules as built

1. **O1.** `bodyStyle` takes the **largest size that holds at least 35 % of the heaviest size's characters** (and is set on at least five lines: see §7), and the face with the most characters **among the lines of that size**. A paper whose heaviest size is its body is unchanged (tested against a copy of the old rule on three shapes). `marginPrintOf`: more than eight rows of small print beyond a page's last full-size line are a section set small (the methods, the references), not a footer; one to a few rows are still margin print. This is the O1 repair's own consequence: the body is now the larger size, so a small-type section can stand below the last full-size line.
2. **O3.** A numbered line is no heading when (a) it is a **street address** (a plain number, capitalised words and a comma, or capitalised words and a postal code; not when set larger than the body), (b) it is the first line of a **reference entry** (number, then authors as a bibliography writes them), (c) its number does not **continue the paper's numbering** (it must come after the last section number: [2, 1] after [2], [3] after [2, 4]; the numbering starts at the paper's first named part, not on the cover), or (d) it stands **inside a references section** (from the heading to the next heading that is set apart) and is not itself set apart. Headings that are set apart move the numbering; a stray line in the body's face is judged against it and moves it only while no set-apart heading has; a set-apart heading that goes back is taken only while the numbering is still the body-face lines'. `bodyStyle` carries the verdicts (`BodyStyle.notHeadings`, keys `page|x|y|text`) so `headingOf`, which `buildOutline` calls line by line and again from the end, answers the same each time; with a plain `{size, font}` body `headingOf` reads as it did.
3. **O4.** A running head is known by its **normalised words** (digits to `#`, case folded) and the band it stands in (top or bottom), on three pages and 40 % of them, **whatever its column or exact height**; `isFurniture` takes a line at a height (12 pt) the head stood at, one head of a row of two columns that another page welds into one line, and the welded row of two heads that other pages split. One-off lines, a head of two pages, a line mid-page and the same words at the top of two pages and the bottom of a third are not furniture. `isFurniture` is now exported.
4. **O7.** `isDropCap` (exported): a line at least 1.8 times the body's size that is one or two letters alone, or opens with a capital and is set in the body's face (the row of the cap and the first words beside it). A **run-in lead-in**: a line smaller than 1.25 times the body, in another face, whose first sentence ends with a period (not an abbreviation) and has more text after it. Neither is a heading; both stay in the text, the lead-in at the start of its paragraph (and no longer welded to the heading above it by `wrapsHeading`).
5. Nothing else changes: captions, columns, section assembly, the paragraph joins.

## 7. For the manager

1. **The drop cap on page 1 is still lost until `titleOf` and the title skip in `buildOutline` know it (P12-51's functions).** With a title present, `titleOf` takes the cap line (28 pt, the largest on the page) as the title, and the main loop's `if (title && line.page === 1 && line.size > body.size * 1.25) continue;` then drops the cap's line from the text; I checked it on an invented page. After my change the cap is no heading, but on page 1 its line does not survive. The fix is `&& !isDropCap(line, body)` in both places (`isDropCap` is exported). My drop-cap test therefore puts the cap on page 2.
2. **Cap and word-rest are one string only when `lineOf` joins them.** A cap whose word goes on flush behind it on its baseline reads whole ("Starting"); a gap wider than 0.18 of the next run's size gives "S tarting", and a cap whose rest is on the row above is not re-joined. That is line formation (`lineOf`, `linesOfRow`, `joinProse`), not mine.
3. **"A next top-level number" is read as "a later one".** Existing tests need it: `pdf-outline.test.ts` takes "6 Acknowledgements" after "1 Introduction" and "20. EXTRAS" after "3.1" (the latter tests the "2." versus "20.1" prefix, so it cannot be re-aimed). A number far ahead (more than 3) is a heading and leaves the numbering where it stood.
4. **Two additions beyond the brief, each forced by an existing test or by the O1 repair:** `BODY_MIN_LINES = 5` (a size must be set on five lines to out-rank the heaviest; `pdf-text.test.ts`'s wrapped-title fixture has three 18 pt title lines that hold 79 % of the characters of its short page) and the margin print row cap (8 rows). The furniture key carries the band (top/bottom) because `pdf-outline.columns.test.ts` has three lines with equal normalised words at different heights on three pages.
5. **`wrapsHeading` and `sectionNumberOf` needed no change.** The lead-in that welded to the heading above it did so through `wrapsHeading`; it is fixed at its source (the lead-in is no heading). The numbering verdicts live in `BodyStyle.notHeadings`, keyed on `page|x|y|text`, so `buildOutline` needs no edit; if the combined `buildOutline` passes `headingOf` a copy of a line whose x, y or text changed, the verdict is lost and the line reads as before (fails open).
6. **Limits:** a numbered reference entry in no author shape, with no references heading set apart before it and a number that continues the numbering, is still a heading; an unnumbered line set apart inside a references section (an italic journal name alone on a line) still is one, and ends the list. The street-address test lets a dotless numbered heading with a comma through when it is set larger than the body.
