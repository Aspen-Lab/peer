## 2. The rules as built

**Rule 1 (`sanitizeReview`, `piece-too-short`).** After the reply has been judged (every earlier check unchanged), the splits it kept are judged part by part:
- A part is judged where `applyCorrections` will find it: from its start after the accepted `move_start`s to the paragraph before the next part's start after them. One helper, `movedStarts`, now serves both (`applyCorrections` calls it; its output is unchanged). A split outside that room is dropped by `applyCorrections` and cuts no piece here.
- The pieces of a part are the runs between its start, its kept splits and its end, whatever their roles. A piece shorter than `REVIEW_CAPS.minPiece` (2) is refused **together with the splits on both sides of it** (the one that begins it, the one that ends it). A pair one paragraph apart is therefore refused whole, in either order of the reply, and the part stays as the draft has it; a lone split at the first, last or any inner position that leaves a one-paragraph piece is refused; a split not next to a short piece stands (splits at 7 and 12 in a part from 6: 7 refused, 12 kept).
- **The exception:** the piece that is the last paragraph of the part (after the moves), when the part is the draft's last evidence part and the split that begins it has the role `interpretation`. The rest of that part is judged as any piece (a two-paragraph last evidence part cannot be split at all).
- **A refused split uses up nothing.** The reply is judged again with the refused splits skipped (at the point where each would have been kept), so a split refused for its piece takes no place among the part's three splits, no repeat memory (a closing asked at the paragraph after a refused non-closing split there still stands), and none of the forty. The rounds end when a round refuses nothing; each round adds at least one of the reply's own splits, so there are at most as many rounds as the reply has corrections.
- Counted under the new reason `piece-too-short` (in `REFUSAL_REASONS`, the closed set), under the op `split`; it adds to `rejected` like every refusal.
- The rule acts on every path that reads a reply, because all of them go through `sanitizeReview`: the route's first pass, its second pass after the looks, and `reviewedParts` in the browser (a review kept before this change is judged by it when laid on the map, as C P12-86's guard is).

**Rule 2 (the numbers).** `sanitizeReview` and `reviewStructure` return `refusedNumbers`: for each of the first `REVIEW_CAPS.refusalsNamed` (8) refused corrections, in the order of the reply, `{ op, reason, part, ordinal, role, draftLength, paragraphs, from, to }`:
- `op` and `reason` from the closed sets; `part` and `ordinal` (`toParagraph` of a move, `atParagraph` of a split, null for `set_role`) are whole numbers or null (the sanitizer's own `whole`: a digit string reads as a number, a float, a negative, prose or an object as null); `role` is one of the five names or null (never the reply's text); `draftLength` is `draft.length`, `paragraphs` the paragraph count; `from` / `to` are the named part's first and last paragraph in the draft (null for a part that does not exist) — for a `piece-too-short` refusal the room it was judged in, after the moves.
- The route writes them on **both** log lines that can carry a refusal: `[papers/structure] unusable` (the fallback, where P4 needs them) and `[papers/structure] review` (a used review, where A P12-57 found `rejected` 1–2 on P6 unnamed). Nothing else of either line changed. No reply text, no paper text, no key can reach it: numbers, closed names and nulls only (pinned by a test that sends prose in every field).

## 7. For the manager

1. **The `review` line carries `refusedNumbers` too.** The brief says "the route's log line" and "its log lines only"; A P12-57 found P6's used reviews with `rejected` 1–2 unnamed (the breakdown existed only for a fallback), so both lines name them. The cost is one pinned key list (`route.test.ts`, re-aimed 3 above). If you want only the `unusable` line, remove the one line in the `review` call and the `refusedNumbers: []` match.
2. **A "piece" is a run between cut points, whatever the roles.** Two adjacent splits of one role (method at 11, method at 12), which would merge into one ten-paragraph method part, are refused like the P6 pair, and so is a no-op split whose role equals the part's own when it cuts a one-paragraph piece. That is the conservative reading of "any piece shorter than 2 paragraphs, counted after the part's other accepted splits". A reading by the final parts (after neighbours of one role merge) would let those through; it costs a dependency on every `set_role` and on the neighbours' roles. Say if you want it.
3. **Not covered, by the brief's wording (splits only):** a `move_start` can still leave a one-paragraph part — the previous part's end moves to a single paragraph (part 1 = 6–10, part 2 moved to 7 leaves part 1 = 6–6), or the moved part itself. The same damage shape, a different correction. A rule for it is a small extension of the same check; not done.
4. **The prompt does not tell the model about the two-paragraph minimum.** Its text is pinned by hash and a change bumps `REVIEW_PROMPT_VERSION` (and so empties every remembered review); outside the brief. A split it asks anyway is refused and counted.
5. **Remembered reviews.** The rule is in `sanitizeReview`, which `reviewedParts` calls, so a review kept in a browser or in the server's memory before this change is judged by it when laid on the map; no entry is invalidated, no version bumped. A cached answer (`cached: true`) still carries the old entry's corrections; the page's laying of it refuses the short piece.
6. **`from` / `to` of a `piece-too-short` entry are the room after the moves**, not the draft's part. For every other reason they are the draft's part. Documented in the type's comment.
