## 2. The rule, as built

**Where.** All of it is in the stack section of `pdf-outline.ts` (`foldStacks` and helpers next to `isStackRow`). Outside that section, three hunks only: two optional fields on `PdfLine` (`reach`, `head`), and the last line of `linesOfRow` (`return line ? [withStackFacts(line, row, ordered)] : []`). `rowsOf`, `lineOf`, `CITED`, `flush()` and `joinProse` are not touched (P9-13b's region; `flush()` needed no change since `foldStacks` is already called from it before `joinProse`).

**Why the line needed two fields.** The fold's `raised` set lives on the `Row`, and by the time `foldStacks` sees a `PdfLine` there is only text. `withStackFacts` (called from `linesOfRow`) adds `reach` (how far the row's runs extend right of `x`, relative, so the right column's x-shift does not matter) and, when the row's first runs are in `row.raised`, `head = { text, rest, reach }`: the raised prefix and the row's words after it, each read through `lineOf` (so they are written exactly as a line is), and the prefix's own reach. Nothing is read from a PDF twice: a row with no raised prefix gets no `head`.

**Rule A, the fraction (`numeratorOf`, `meets`, a branch of `foldStacks` after the P9-09 stack-run and sandwich branches).** At a text row, when the line above is unfinished (no `. ! ? : ;`), the row has a `head`, the row itself is no stack row (so P9-09's stack-run branch keeps whatever it took before), the head is a fragment (<= 40 characters, no run of four letters, a letter or digit in it) that is **not** only digits, separators and signs (`MARK_ONLY`), the words after it open with an operator (`STACK_OPERATOR`, as P9-09's sandwich requires), and the next row is a fragment row within 0.8 x leading below whose x-range meets the head's: the two rows become one `FlowLine` `"<head> / <fragment> <rest>"` (`continues`, `x` of the line above, `y` of the denominator, as the sandwich does), and `joinProse` joins it into the sentence above.

**Rule B, the other half (same loop, after A).** A fragment row that is not part of a stack, tight (0.8 x leading) under an unfinished row that has no `head`, with a lower-case line after it, is emitted `continues`: it joins the sentence with a space and never opens a paragraph.

**Two narrowings the brief did not spell out, both mine, both for precision:**
1. A raised head of only digits, separators and signs (`2`, `2+`, `−1`, `12`, `3-5`) is never a numerator. The brief's "at least one letter or digit" would take them, and the guard "a raised exponent / a raised citation at a row's start is not a fraction" needs them refused: `lineOf` keeps such a mark at a row's head (nothing is before it to be a citation of), so P9-08's drop rule cannot be what refuses it. The cost: a purely numeric numerator (`1` over `n`) stays as the page set it.
2. The words after the head must open with an operator. The brief's text for the fold case names the head and the fragment row, but P9-09's sandwich and its surviving test ("leaves a script-like label around a prose row alone: the row must open with an operator") make the operator the thing that tells a fraction from a label above and below a prose row; a raised `g(ab)` ahead of "μm was observed at room temperature" with a fragment under it is not a fraction (guard test, red when the operator check is removed).

P9-09's sandwich (numerator more than 7.5 pt above, so not folded) is untouched and still green: the same sentence, the same words, from the other geometry.
