## 2. The rules as built

**1. The damage guard (`sanitizeReview`).** The paper's opening run is the draft's leading parts of role `setup`; `openingEnd` is the index of the first part that is not one (the draft's length when every part is setup). Judged against the draft as given, like every other check:
- a `set_role` to `setup` is refused for any part **after** `openingEnd` (`k > openingEnd`). The first part that is not setup may become setup: that moves the opening's end, which the brief lets through;
- a `split` whose new piece has role `setup` is refused in the first part that is not setup and in every part after it (`k >= openingEnd`): a piece cut out of the middle of a part cannot continue the opening. This is the P6 case, and it is the reading the brief's own measure needs (a split of the first evidence part, which is the first non-setup part, must be refused). A split inside the opening run is judged as before;
- a refused correction is counted in `rejected` with the others and does not use up the part's three splits or its repeat memory (a test pins it).
Every other correction is judged exactly as before: a differential fuzz of 60,000 random drafts and replies (scratch, not committed) found the version before the guard and the guard's identical on all 30,000 cases with no `setup` role in them, and on the 30,000 that had one, the 1,325 that differ differ only in a correction with the role `setup` (1,411 guard refusals in all); no accepted `setup` correction lies after the opening. The same fuzz found the rule-2 sanitizer identical to the rule-1 one in `answer`, `rejected` and `read` on all 60,000, and each tally adding up to `rejected`.
The guard acts on every path that reads a reply, because all of them go through `sanitizeReview`: the route's first pass, the route's second pass after the looks (`resolveLooks` can add a `set_role` from a look verdict), and `reviewedParts` in the browser (so a review kept before this change is judged by it when laid over the map).

**2. The fallback says why, as counts only.**
- `reviewStructure` returns three new fields: `cause` (`null` unless it fell back; else `not-json` — the reply is not an object with a `corrections` array; `all-refused` — it held corrections and every one was refused; `unsound` — what the corrections made was not a sound structure, or the step threw), `read` (corrections in the reply, 0 for `not-json`) and `refused: { byOp, byReason }` (only the non-zero keys).
- `sanitizeReview` returns the same `refused`. Ops are `move_start`, `set_role`, `split`, and `other` (not an object, or an op outside the three); reasons are `malformed`, `out-of-range`, `part-0`, `same-role`, `repeat`, `split-cap`, `setup-after-opening`, `over-cap`. Both are closed sets (`REFUSAL_OPS`, `REFUSAL_REASONS`); the reply's own words are never a key. `malformed` and `over-cap` are mine, so that every refusal has a name and the tallies add up to `rejected` (the brief's list had six).
- The route's `[papers/structure] unusable` debug line is now `{ promptChars, answerChars, cause, read, rejected, refusedByOp, refusedByReason }`. Nothing else in the route changed; the `review` line is untouched (its pinned key list is unchanged).

## 7. For the manager

1. **A decision to check: the first non-setup part may still become setup.** The brief says the guard refuses setup "to anything after the draft's first part whose role is not setup", so `set_role` of that first part itself passes (it moves the opening's end, like a `move_start`). In a draft `setup | evidence | interpretation` that lets a single `set_role part 1 → setup` turn the whole results part into setup. It draws a second look (a correction against a `high` or `medium` part) but not a refusal. To close it, change `k > openingEnd` to `k >= openingEnd` in the `set_role` branch (one token; one test of the guard, "still applies ... the first part after the opening", then changes).
