## What was built (brief items 1–6)

1. **Prompt rules** (`lib/papers/explain.ts`). `EXPLAIN_BREVITY_RULES` — seven sentences, pinned word for word: the fewest words that are still exact; no preamble; never restate the question or say where the passage sits (the box already shows it); no general background beyond what the passage needs; one idea per sentence; quote the author's own phrase exactly as the text supplied has it; write in the reader's language. They are in the system prompt of the first answer and of every reply. The reply prompt names the cap that applies: "at most three sentences and 560 characters … the reader has not asked for more", or with `detail` "at most eight sentences and 1,400 characters … the reader asked for more"; `detail` changes the cap and nothing else (a test compares the two prompts part by part). The reply schema gains the optional `items` with its own description (at most four rows of `{ term, here, read }`, a dozen words a cell, only when the reader asks about several terms or quantities) and a rule that with `items` the reply is at most two sentences.
2. **Caps and the detail path.** `EXPLAIN_CAPS` gains `replyDetailSentences: 8`, `replyDetailChars: 1400`, `itemRows: 4`, `itemWords: 12`, `itemChars: 80`, `itemsReplySentences: 2`. `sanitizeExplainReply(raw, { detail })` (and the first answer's two parts, which share the helper) keeps the leading whole sentences that fit both caps (`wholeSentences`) and nothing of the rest. Pure `asksForDetail(message)`: detail, detailed, in depth / in-depth, elaborate, expand, step by step, tell me more, say more, more detail; 详细, 展开, 具体, 深入, 多说, 讲讲; whole words and case-insensitive for English (letter/digit look-arounds, so "retail", "detailing", "expanded" and "elaborately" are not matches); "are there more papers?" and a bare "more" are not requests. Route: `detail` read from the body (only a literal `true`), `detail = replying && (body.detail === true || asksForDetail(last reader message))` (the first answer has no long form; a first message carrying `detail: true` is answered as ever); `explainCacheKey(docHash, passage, thread, searched, detail)` adds `|detail` only when true, so every existing key is unchanged and a short and a long reply are two entries (one model call each, one charge each, each a hit on its own repeat; asking in words and pressing the button are the same entry). The turn carries `detail: true` when long. Token budget: the long form asks for 800 (the default reply keeps its 400, so the two existing pins of 400 stand). The charge is untouched (one tenth, ten when searched; a test pins both with `detail`). The debug line gained no key (the existing exact-keys tests stand) and no word of the reader's.
3. **The term table.** `sanitizeItems` (the shape): at most four rows, each of `term`, `here`, `read`, each cell at most twelve words and eighty characters, a row over a cap or with a missing cell dropped and never cut, a term already in the table not repeated, a web address taken out of a cell; with rows present the prose is at most two sentences (default or detail alike). `groundExplainItems` + `verifyExplainReply(reply, doc, preferSectionId, scope)` (the grounding drop): a row stays only when its term occurs, after `normalizeForMatch` on both sides, in the passage, its paragraph, the neighbours or the located section; without the scope given no row is kept; with every row dropped the turn has no `items`. The thread store's `ExplainTurn` carries `items` and `detail` (plain fields, persisted and restored like the rest; old threads are unchanged). `explain-thread.ts` reads `items` (rows that are three cells of words, at most four — `MAX_EXPLAIN_ITEMS`, pinned equal to the server's cap) and `detail` (a literal `true`) off the server's turn. The box draws `<table>`: three `scope="col"` headers in the label face (`EXPLAIN.tableTerm` "Term", `tableHere` "Here it means", `tableRead` "How to read it", all in `copy.ts`), cells in the reading face a step smaller than the prose (`text-body-sm`) so three columns fit the 360 px card, under the reply's prose and above its quote, never quote-styled, no link. The first answer is unchanged.
4. **"Say more"** (`EXPLAIN.sayMore` = "Say more"). One label-face button (`data-explain-say-more`) under Peer's latest reply, drawn by `ExplainCard` only when the box has `onSayMore`, the last turn is Peer's, a reader message precedes it and the reply is not already long; never under the first answer. Disabled while a request is in flight, at the full thread and when the day's allowance is spent (`sendBlocked`, the send's own rule). **How I did it (the brief asked me to say):**
   - `sayMoreOf(turns)` returns the reader's **last message** and the turns **before** it. The box sends `thread = [firstAnswerMessage, ...before]` and `message = that last reader message` through `onSayMore`; `requestReply` appends the message once, so the request is the thread *before* the message and the message — **no duplicate reader message, and the reply to be lengthened is left out**. Sent that way the thread holds exactly the reader messages it held, so the server's `readers` rule (more than eight is full) and its rule that a thread ends with the reader's message both hold; the flow test and a route test (eight readers, `detail: true`, 200) pin it.
   - The reply is appended as **Peer's next turn alone** (`moreReply`: no reader message, so the reader count does not grow and nothing is replaced); the earlier short reply stays on screen; what the reader was typing stays in the input; a failed one leaves the thread as it was and says so; an allowance refusal behaves as a send's.
   - It goes through the page's own new callback `moreExplain` → `requestReply({ …, detail: true })` (never `search`), and `addExplainTurns(…, moreReply(result))`. I made it a callback beside `replyExplain` rather than a fifth argument of it so the typed send, and its source-text pins in `page.explain.test.ts`, are untouched.
   - **A consequence the brief did not foresee, and its fix.** Extra Peer turns would push a full thread past the server's 17 messages (first answer + 8 pairs): at eight readers with a Say more on each, the next send would carry up to 25 messages, and a longer thread is *no thread*, so the route would silently answer a first answer the client reads as "unavailable". `threadAsSent` (used by `requestReply`) keeps one Peer message per reader message — a Peer turn that another Peer turn follows is left out and the long reply stands for it, the model seeing what the reader last read. A test holds a thread of eight readers each with a short and a long reply to 15 messages, and the page-level pin that the typed send is unchanged stays green.
5. **The harness** (`describe("brevity harness")` in `explain.test.ts`): **11 over-long canned replies** (English and Chinese; default and detail; lectures of short sentences, sentences of 200–300 characters, a quotation with full stops in it, and two sentences that never stop), **4 over-long first answers** (English and Chinese) and **2 tables** (seven rows with paragraph-sized cells under four sentences; a Chinese table), through `sanitizeExplainReply` / `sanitizeExplainAnswer` — every output within the caps (3/560, 8/1,400, 2×420, ≤4 rows × 12 words × 80 characters, prose ≤2 sentences with a table), every sentence whole (the output is a prefix of the cleaned input that ends where a sentence of it ends) and quotation marks balanced; plus "detail is never shorter than default" and a model that ignores every rule at once. ## Where the brief was silent, or I read it (for the manager)

1. **Files.** The brief's file list omits `page.tsx` and `page.explain.test.ts`; §3b's P3 row allows both, and "re-sends … through `requestReply`" cannot happen without the page's callback. The page change is `moreExplain` + one prop + `moreReply` in the import; the existing import pin in `page.explain.test.ts` now names `moreReply` (rewritten, see below).
2. **"A quote is never cut."** I read it as: the sentence cap applies to the prose, never to the quote, and a quotation *inside* the prose is never cut in half (the sentences of an open quotation are one group; one that does not fit whole is left out; a quotation the text never closes holds nothing together). The reply's `evidence` keeps the pre-existing P3-02 bound of 400 characters (a defensive slice before the verifier), untouched: cutting a long verified quote to its sentence boundary or marking the cut is BACKLOG-13 (§1h.8 (8)) in P4 and I did not start it.
3. **A single sentence over the cap.** "Never mid-sentence" cannot hold when the first sentence alone is longer than the cap, short of returning no reply (which an existing test, "caps a part's length", rules out and which would cost the reader the answer and the charge). I cut that one sentence at a word and end it with "…": a cut is never silent (the same house rule as §1h.8 (8)). It is the only place Peer's words end inside a sentence; the harness asserts it for English and Chinese. If the manager prefers no reply there, it is one line in `wholeSentences`.
4. **Chinese.** `splitSentences` only knows `. ! ?` before a space, so a Chinese answer was one "sentence". `wholeSentences` splits first on 。！？ (and on the ASCII `!`/`?` that `cleanDisplayText` makes of the full-width ones, when Chinese text follows), then with the existing Latin splitter. A table cell's "words" count Chinese or Japanese characters two to a word (a dozen words ≈ two dozen characters; never looser than the 80-character cap).
5. **`cleanDisplayText` turns `f_x` into `fx`.** It reads a bare underscore as a LaTeX subscript. For an exact answer that quotes the author's own notation this is a visible fault, so `words()` and the table cells keep the underscore (`tidy`: protect, clean, restore; a test pins it for the reply, the first answer and a table term). **Not fixed, flagged:** the reply's and the first answer's `evidence` is still cleaned (the displayed verified quote reads `fx`; the verifier is unaffected because it cleans both sides) — it belongs with BACKLOG-13, the one-verifier item.
6. **Grounding is whole-word.** The ruling says the term must occur after `normalizeForMatch` on both sides; I also require it not to sit inside a longer word or number ("auge" is not in "gauge", "0.5" is not in "10.55"), for Latin, Greek, Cyrillic letters and digits; Chinese has no boundaries and none is applied. It is stricter than a bare `includes` and only ever drops a row (the table is shorter, never wrong). The shape cap (first four fit rows) is applied before grounding, so a row the paper does not hold still uses one of the four slots; the order the model wrote is the order kept.
7. **Say more is hidden under a reply that is already long** (`detail: true` on the turn). Pressed there it would ask the same question again, a cache hit that appends a duplicate turn. This is where the turn's `detail` is used: it is set by the server for the button and for a request that asks in words alike.
8. **The system prompts' line "Never quote the paper except with a sentence copied character-for-character"** became "…except with words copied…": the new rule has Peer quote the author's own phrase, which the old line forbade. Nothing pinned the old wording.
9. **First-answer parts.** The ruling says the first answer is unchanged in shape; its two parts now share the whole-sentence cut (they were cut at a word at 420 characters, mid-sentence), because the harness is to hold "the sanitizers" to whole sentences. Their caps (two sentences, 420 characters) are as they were.
10. **`maxTokens`.** The long form asks for 800; the default reply stays at 400. A full four-row table with every cell at the cap and a quote could exceed 400 tokens and be cut mid-JSON (the reply would then be "unavailable"); a typical table of two or three rows is well inside it. I did not raise it because two existing tests pin 400 and I cannot measure a real model here — A may want to see it with a real key.
