## 0. In one paragraph

A left click on a Paper-digestion figure does **nothing** today (the picture is a bare `<img>`: cursor `auto`, no button, no handler). The Summary's
figure is a full-window modal lightbox with exactly **two** zoom states (fit, and 2x), a click to toggle, a drag when zoomed, Escape and an X; **no wheel, no
+/- keys, no zoom buttons**. The design: the figure becomes a button; a click opens **one figure card in the right margin** (kind `"figure"` in the same
windows stack the stickies use), filling the margin's width and the height under the masthead when it is the only card, with a header (label, caption
head, **X at the top right**), a slim zoom row (minus, readout, plus, Fit), the picture on its mat (wheel zoom at the pointer, click toggle fit/2x as in the
Summary, drag to pan), and the caption under it (scrolls, at most 30 % of the card). The zoom/pan/pointer code the lightbox owns is **extracted into a hook
both use**; the Summary's lightbox keeps its two steps and no wheel, so it behaves exactly as measured below. Below the margin's width the figure opens
in **today's lightbox overlay** (the very same viewer). Three disjoint Cs (section 6); `page.tsx` gets one one-line hunk; the Summary does not change
(the owner, P10 review item 6: "summary做得很好，不用再更改了").

## 2. The design

### 2.1 What the reader sees

1. **The click.** In Paper digestion every figure whose picture is on screen is a button (`aria-label="Open Figure 3 in the margin"`, `cursor-zoom-in`,
   `aria-expanded` while its card is open, a quiet accent outline while open). A figure whose picture did not arrive (the caption stands alone, `paper-body.tsx:643,673`
   `showImage`) has no button — nothing to open.
2. **The card.** One figure card in the margin, a column window of kind `"figure"`. Alone it fills the margin: width = the margin's width, from the masthead's foot
   (`DOCK_TOP_PX` 48) to the column's foot (`columnBottomPx`: 960 at 1440 x 1000, so **912 px tall**; 680 at 1280 x 720, **632 px**). Top to bottom:
   - **Header, 40 px** (the stickies' header): the label ("Figure 3") in the eyebrow face, the caption's first words truncated in italic muted, and the **X at the top
     right** (40 x 40, `aria-label="Close Figure 3"`). The whole header (but the X) is the button that brings the card forward (as a sticky's).
   - **Zoom row, 36 px**: minus, the readout ("100 %", "200 %"), plus, **Fit** (reset zoom and pan). Disabled at the ends.
   - **The picture**, on the plate mat (`--plate-mat`, light in both themes, as the body's and the lightbox's), centred, fitted by `clampFigureSize` into the card's own
     measured box (ResizeObserver, as `FigureViewer` does at `:55-68`; so dragging the divider re-fits live). The pointer surface is a `<button>` exactly like the
     lightbox's: **click toggles fit <-> 2x** (the inherited behaviour), **drag pans** when zoomed (cursor grab / grabbing), **wheel zooms at the pointer**
     (plain wheel and Ctrl/Cmd + wheel, which is also a trackpad pinch), keys `+ = - 0` and the arrows while the focus is on it.
   - **The caption** under the picture, the whole of it (the lightbox shows the caption whole; the body's `figcaption` too), in the reading face, `overflow-y-auto`,
     at most 30 % of the card's height, `overscroll-contain`. At 1440 x 1000 that is up to about 270 px (the owner's longest caption, 1761 characters, is about 40
     lines at this width, so it scrolls).
3. **Zoom range**: fit (1x) to **6x** of fit; button steps `[1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6]`; the click toggle goes fit <-> `DETAIL_ZOOM` (2x); wheel is continuous
   (`exp(-deltaY * 0.0015)`, `deltaMode` normalised) anchored at the pointer. Pan clamped by `clampFigurePan`; re-clamped when the card resizes or the caption's height
   changes.
4. **Escape** closes the figure card **wherever the focus is** (see 2.4), the X closes it, a second figure click **replaces** it (same card, new picture, zoom
   and pan reset to fit; it comes forward), the same figure clicked again keeps it open and brings it forward (no toggle-off: a stray click must not drop a zoomed view).
5. **Nothing persists**: a reload shows no card; the card does not follow the reader to the next paper (`key={paper.id}` and an unmount cleanup, as `StickyNotes`).

### 2.2 How the lightbox's math and pointer code are shared (C1)

Today everything lives in `FigureViewer`'s body (`figure-lightbox.tsx:35-217`): the state (`zoomed`, `pan`, `dragging`, `drag`, `suppressClick`, `:47-51`), the math
(`size`, `scale`, `offset`, `:103-111`), `toggleZoom` (`:113-120`) and the three pointer handlers (`:122-151`). Extract **all of that and nothing else** into:

- `web/src/components/reader/figure-zoom.ts` (new, plain module, no React): `DETAIL_ZOOM`, `clampFigureSize`, `clampFigurePan` **moved here and re-exported from
  `figure-lightbox.tsx`** (`figure-lightbox.test.ts` imports them from `./figure-lightbox`), plus the new pure helpers `FIGURE_ZOOM_STEPS = [1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6]`,
  `nextZoom(zoom, steps, direction)`, `zoomAbout(pan, zoom, nextZoom, anchor)` (keeps the point under the pointer still: `anchor - (anchor - pan) * next / zoom`, anchor
  measured from the viewport's centre, since the picture's transform origin is its centre) and `wheelFactor(event)`.
- `web/src/components/reader/use-figure-zoom.ts` (new hook):

```ts
export function useFigureZoom(cfg: {
  natural: Size | null;                 // the picture's own size; null until known -> size {0,0}
  viewport: Size;                       // the box it is fitted into and panned inside (already net of padding / clearance)
  steps?: readonly number[];            // default FIGURE_ZOOM_STEPS; the lightbox passes [1, DETAIL_ZOOM]
  wheel?: boolean;                      // default false; the card passes true
}): {
  size: Size; scale: number; offset: Point; zoomed: boolean; dragging: boolean;
  canZoomIn: boolean; canZoomOut: boolean;
  zoomIn(): void; zoomOut(): void; reset(): void; toggle(): void;     // toggle = the lightbox's click: fit <-> DETAIL_ZOOM, pan reset
  surface: { onClick; onPointerDown; onPointerMove; onPointerUp; onPointerCancel; onLostPointerCapture };  // spread on the <button>
  surfaceRef: (el: HTMLElement | null) => void;                        // native wheel {passive:false} and keydown, when wheel / keys are on
  style: CSSProperties;                                                // { width, height, transform: translate(offset) scale(scale) } — byte-identical to today's
}
```

`FigureViewer` then calls `useFigureZoom({ natural, viewport, steps: [1, DETAIL_ZOOM], wheel: false })` and spreads `surface` / `style`; **its markup, labels, the
`ResizeObserver`, the keydown effect (`:70-101`), the focus handling and the portal stay where they are, character for character.** The keydown effect must
stay in `figure-lightbox.tsx`: two tests read its source text (section 7). `FigureViewer` gains `export` (one word) so the overlay fallback (section 4) can mount it.

Two facts the hook must respect:

- **React registers `wheel` (and `touchstart`/`touchmove`) as passive listeners at the root** (react-dom 19.2.4, `react-dom-client.development.js:19251-19255`), so a React
  `onWheel` cannot `preventDefault` and the page would scroll / the browser would page-zoom under a pinch. The wheel handler must be a native `addEventListener("wheel", h,
  { passive: false })` on the surface (via `surfaceRef`), attached only when `wheel` is true. There is no `onWheel` or `passive: false` anywhere in `src` today.
- The global keyboard layer binds `ArrowRight` / `ArrowLeft` to next / previous paper and Ctrl/Cmd `+` / `-` to the reading scale (`reader-keys.ts:39-40`, `keyboard.tsx:132-150`).
  While the focus is on the card's surface the hook consumes `+ = - 0` and the four arrows (`stopPropagation`, `preventDefault`; arrows pan when zoomed and are swallowed
  at fit, so a reader who focused the figure never leaves the paper by accident) and **never** a key with Ctrl/Cmd (the reading scale keeps working).

### 2.3 The card, the store, the host (C2, C3)

- **Store** `web/src/lib/reader/figure-viewer.ts` (new, plain module, zustand like `useWindows`; C3): `useFigureViewer: { figure: OpenFigure | null, mode: "card" | "overlay" }`,
  `OpenFigure = { key, src, label, caption, width?, height?, page?, origin?: HTMLElement | null }` (`key` = `${label}:${ordinal}`, the body's own React key, `paper-body.tsx:832`),
  `openFigure(f, scroll?)`, `closeFigure(scroll?)`, `useFigureOpen(key)`. `openFigure` decides `mode` from the window's width at that moment
  (>= the margin's minimum: card; below: overlay), and in card mode does `holdInPlace({ measure: () => origin top, change: () => useWindows.getState().open({ id: "figure",
  kind: "figure", title: f.label }) })` — **the same call `takeStickyNote` makes** (`sticky-note.tsx:231-235`): the first window of the column reserves the page's width, the text
  reflows (at 1440 the column moved 184 px, at 1280 it narrowed by 168 px), and the figure the reader clicked stays where their eyes are. `useWindows.open` on an id that
  stands already **retitles and raises** it (`windows.ts:44-48`): that IS "a second figure replaces the first". In memory only; nothing persisted, no URL logged.
- **Card** `web/src/components/reader/figure-card.tsx` (new; C2): `FigureCardView` (stateless, like `StickyWindowView`: props `label, caption, src, natural, front, top, height, z,
  onClose, onRaise` plus the hook's outputs) and `FigureCard` (stateful: owns `useFigureZoom`, keeps **zoom and pan in this wrapper, which stays mounted while the card is behind**, so a
  header press brings it back forward exactly as left; reset on a different `key`). `role="region"`, `aria-label="Figure 3"`, **no `aria-modal`** (the explain window's rule,
  `explain-box.tsx:528-535`: a non-modal card must not trip `MODAL_OVER_PAGE` and shut its own and Explain's Escape out). `fixed right-0 w-[min(360px,100vw)] ... z` from the stack,
  like `sticky-note.tsx:81-82`; the body's size is **measured from its own box** (ResizeObserver), never assumed 360 — that is what lets P11-01's draggable margin and P11-03's stack
  give it any box.
- **Host** `web/src/components/reader/figure-host.tsx` (new; C2): mounted once per paper (`key={paper.id}`); reads the store; in card mode renders `FigureCard` at the row the stack gives
  the `"figure"` window (`stackRows`, `useColumn`, as `StickyNotes` does); in overlay mode renders a portal `FigureViewer` (section 4); registers the Escape listener (2.4); closes the
  card when the window narrows below the margin's minimum; closes on unmount (the paper changed). Focus: on open the card's surface takes the focus (`preventScroll`) so `+ - 0`, the
  arrows and Escape work at once; on close focus returns to the body's figure button if it is still connected (the lightbox does the same with `previousFocus`, `:71-99`).

### 2.4 Escape, keys, focus (the sharp edges)

- The page's own Escape is "back to the briefing" and runs on `window` in the bubble phase (`keyboard.tsx:306`). A card that only listens when the focus is inside it (sticky's React
  `onKeyDown`, `sticky-note.tsx:75-80`) would let a reader who just clicked a figure press Escape with the focus in the text and **leave the paper**. So the figure card
  also registers, while it is open, **one `document` bubble listener exactly like the explain window's** (`explain-box.tsx:879-905`): it returns on `defaultPrevented` and on
  `document.querySelector(MODAL_OVER_PAGE)`, else `preventDefault` + `stopPropagation` + close. Bubble on `document` runs before the keyboard layer's `window` listener.
- **One Escape, one thing.** With the figure card and the explain window both open, two document listeners would both fire. Contract with P11-03 (A3 below): **only the card in front closes on
  Escape**; each card's listener is gated on "I am the front window" (`useWindows` order). The explain box's listener needs the same one-line gate (P11-03's file).
- Keys inside the card: section 2.2. Tab is not trapped (non-modal); the order is header press, X, minus, plus, Fit, surface, caption.

## 4. Below the margin's width

P11-01 decides the number; assumed 1024 (`DOCK_MIN_VIEWPORT_PX`, `explain-dock.ts:48`; the Explain dock, the notes tab and the stack's reserve all use it). **Recommendation: below it, the
figure opens in today's lightbox overlay** — the same `FigureViewer` the Summary uses, mounted by the host in a portal with `natural` read from the clicked picture (`img.naturalWidth` /
`naturalHeight`, as `FigureLightbox.open` does, `:235-239`; the body's `Figure` already holds the `img` through `onPicture`). Reasons: it exists and is tested; it is modal and full-window, which
is right where there is no margin to fill; a sheet is a new surface to build and to test for no gain on a 768-1023 px laptop window. No reserve, no stack, nothing persists. When a window is
narrowed below the minimum while a card is open the card **closes** (nothing is persistent below the dock's width, `explain-dock.ts:36`); it does not jump into an overlay. Under 768 px
nothing changes from the overlay (the touch pointer code already works: `touch-none`, pointer capture).

## 5. Contracts this design assumes of the other items (to reconcile)

- **A1 (P11-01, the margin pane)**: the card sizes itself to whatever box it is given and measures that box itself; it needs only the margin's left edge, top and width, and the height down to
  the column's foot. Today that is `fixed right-0`, `top = 48`, `width 360`, `bottom = columnBottomPx(...)`; if the margin becomes `[data-reader-margin]` the card is
  `absolute inset-0` in it or keeps `fixed` at its rect. Opening a card **expands the margin if it is collapsed** (a reader who pressed a figure wants to see it).
- **A2 (P11-01)**: one exported minimum width for "a margin exists" (assumed 1024). If the margin becomes a permanent pane, `reserveDock` / `--explain-dock` go away and `holdInPlace` is a harmless
  no-op (it measures, finds no movement, scrolls nothing). The figure's fit width equals the margin's width less 16: at 360 about 330 px (section 1.3); each 100 px the divider gives is 100 px of
  figure. A default margin of about 420-480 would be kinder to the owner's figures (D6).
- **A3 (P11-03, the stack)**: cards are `sticky | notes | explain | figure` (| a highlight's); each has a 40-px header (title, X) and a body; one is forward and takes all remaining height
  (floor 240 for notes and figure); opening a card brings it forward; a header press raises it; Escape closes only the forward card. `useWindows` stays the store; **this item needs only these hunks
  in `windows.ts`** (P11-03's file): `WindowKind` + `"figure"` (`:17`); a "tall front" test for notes **and** figure in `stackLayout` (`:119-125`) and `stackRoom` (`:153-160`) with `FIGURE_MIN_FRONT_PX = 240`.
  If the stack is rewritten as a component that renders a card per window, `FigureHost`'s card is rendered by it for kind `"figure"` and C2's `page.tsx` hunk is dropped.
- **A4 (P11-03)**: the selection's action buttons are above the cards (z) and never cover a header; "explain" and "figure" Escapes are each gated on "I am the front card".
- **A5 (P11-04, highlight)**: independent. If a highlight gets a card it is one more kind in the same stack; nothing here depends on it.
