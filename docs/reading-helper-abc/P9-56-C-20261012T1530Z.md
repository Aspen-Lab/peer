## 2. The rules as built (ruling §1h.73 (b))

1. **The opening is the paragraph's first (non-boilerplate) sentence, whole, when it is at most 400 characters.** `OPENING_MAX` 160 → 400; the constant's comment says why (the owner's paper puts 92 % of its first sentences under it: 56 of 61 long paragraphs, median 207, p90 372, max 735; an opening is read inside a row's fold). The growth rule is unchanged and now runs to the new cap: a first sentence under 40 characters still takes the following whole sentences, while the total stays at most 400; a later sentence that would pass 400 is left out whole.
2. **A first sentence over 400 is cut at a word with one "…"** (`cutFirstSentence`), the `leadCaption` rule: the kept text ends at the last white space the room reaches, the trailing run of `[white space , ; : . ! ? - – — ( [` is dropped, then one "…". The room is 399 characters, so **the line, mark included, is at most 400**. An unbroken run with no white space in the room (a URL) is cut hard at 399 and still marked; a cut between the halves of a surrogate pair keeps neither; a run of nothing but marks falls back to the room as it stands (never a lone "…"). The stop of an abbreviation the cut happens to land on ("et al.") goes like any other: "…Okafor et al…".
3. **Everything else as it was:** where a sentence ends is still `splitSentences` (`skim.ts`: "Fig.", "et al.", initials, a lower-case continuation); the starts are as they were (boilerplate first sentences are skipped; no start rule of X9 lived in this function and none was added); a formula's introduction (a line ending on ":" with no stop, no more than 400 characters) is whole.

Types and comments: `ReadingMapParagraph.opening` now says "a verbatim paragraph substring, plus that mark when it is cut" (it was "always a verbatim paragraph substring"). The mark is carried in the string, as the brief asks; `reading-map.tsx` needed no change (`MathText` draws "…" as plain text).

## 8. For the manager

1. **Not measured in a browser.** The view is unchanged, so I drove no dev server. The cost of the rule is height: an opened row's line is now up to 400 characters (about 2.5 times the old 160) where its first sentence is long; rows are folded by default. The next walk should read the opened map's height at 390 and 1024 (X3's volume).
2. **A colon-ended introduction over 400 is cut.** The brief's rule 3 says "a line ending on ':' that introduces an island is whole". I read that as whole when it is at most 400 (the walk's two introductions are 57 and 639 characters; the 639 one becomes a "…" line). If it should stay whole at any length, that is a one-line exception in `openingOf` (a first sentence ending in ":" is returned as it is) and a ruling.
3. **A cut can still land inside an inline formula.** `⟦h_{t} + 1⟧` has spaces inside the TeX, the cut may fall between them, and `splitMath` then prints the unclosed "⟦" as text before the "…". It was so at 160 (and was more likely); it is rare at 400. Not in the brief; the fix would back the cut up to before the open bracket (`kept.lastIndexOf(MATH_OPEN) > kept.lastIndexOf(MATH_CLOSE)`), in the same function.
4. **One difference from `leadCaption`.** `leadCaption` backs off to a hard cut when the last word boundary is in the first half of the room. I kept the head's rule (the last word boundary however early; a hard cut only with no white space at all), as "everything else as it is" and "cut at a word" ask: a first sentence that is a 450-character token after a three-letter word would read "See…".
5. **A stop dropped by the cut.** `splitSentences` joins a piece that starts in lower case to the sentence before it, so "context. xxx…" is one sentence to `openingOf`; if the cap then falls right after "context." the line reads "context…" (stop stripped, by rule 2), not "context." Right by the rule, but it is the one way a real sentence end can lose its stop.
6. **Model-facing consumers, not mine, unchanged and green.** `explain.ts` still clips each map line it sends the model to `EXPLAIN_CAPS.openingChars` 160 with its own bare `cutAtWord` (never drawn on the page); `paragraph-guide.ts` sends the opening as `topicSentence` uncut, so the model now sees up to 400 characters and a closing "…" when the sentence was cut, and its doc comment "the paper's words, verbatim" is now "plus a mark when cut".
7. **Rubric reading.** With 0 bare cuts the Theme-1 pass requirement "the map's paragraph openings whole sentences" is met in the letter wherever the first sentence is at most 400 characters, and a longer one is visibly cut, which is what ruling §1h.73 (b) settles; N9 (0.25) should be gone on the next walk.
