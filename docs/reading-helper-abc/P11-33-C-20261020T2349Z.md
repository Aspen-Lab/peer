(Sweep: the inner box's `max-width` overridden on the live page at 1920 / 2560 wide, 16 widths from 416 to 2,000 px; "of N" where the paper has fewer than 30 non-final lines at that width, so the wide rows are a small sample. No sampled width up to 1,700 px passes on both papers.) The reason is arithmetic, not a bug: a greedy line's slack can be as wide as the next word, and Chromium shares it among the line's gaps, so a 15-letter word costs a 3- to 6-space gap in a line of eight gaps and a 30-letter one costs more at any width a reader's column has.

**Does `hyphens: auto` break words on the page?** No, and a `lang` is not why. The root says `<html lang="en">` (`layout.tsx`, held by a test), and the paragraphs compute `hyphens: auto` (12 of 12). `hyphens: auto` needs a hyphenation dictionary, and the Chromium the measure runs in has none: a probe (`h/hyph.mjs`) of a 136 px word in a 90 px box under `lang="en"; hyphens: auto` stays one line and overflows, while the same word with soft hyphens breaks. So fixing the `lang` alone does nothing (there is nothing to fix), and a browser that does have the dictionary was not available to measure. An **upper bound** for what any dictionary could do: soft hyphens put at every position that leaves two letters each side of every word of 5+ letters (`h/tw.mjs`), at the first 30 lines:

So even a perfect dictionary does not make 416 meet the bar, and Chromium's `text-wrap: pretty` makes the hyphenation worse (it prefers a wide gap to a hyphen): with the dictionary and `wrap`, 646 would meet it on both papers. ## The rule and the means

`@utility reading-justify` keeps its three declarations first (`text-align: justify; hyphens: auto; text-wrap: pretty`, so `explain-box.test.tsx`'s pin on its head is green unchanged) and gains two nested rules, each setting only `text-align: left`:

```css
@container reader-centre (width < 38.5em) { text-align: left; }
@media (width < 64rem)                    { text-align: left; }
```

`.reader-centre-inner` gains `container: reader-centre / inline-size;` and nothing else.

- **Means: a container query on the centre's inner box**, not the pane (`.reader-centre`). The inner box is the text's own width with the 38em cap applied; the pane's content box would read 712 px at 1440 where the text is 646, and so could not see the cap. It is **named** (`reader-centre`), so another `@container` on the page (the plate cards use one) cannot answer it. `em` in the condition is the container's own 17 px, so Fit's `zoom` (which scales box and type together) gives the same answer: at 1920 with Fit on the inner box is 645.99 local px and is ragged.
- **Why 38.5em.** The shell draws the box at 416 px (the 440 px centre minimum less its padding) up to the 38em cap (646 px) and never wider, and the measure says none of those widths can be justified, so the switch sits just above the cap: every column the shell has is ragged. A column wider than the cap would keep S8's justification (none exists today; the data above do not promise it passes, which is why the switch is not placed nearer the 1,350 to 1,800 px where two of the invented papers' samples happened to pass). A test ties the two numbers: `38.5em > the cap read from `.reader-centre-inner``, so raising the cap is a decision to measure again.
- **The one-column page** (below 64rem, `SPREAD_QUERY`; the shell is not mounted) has no container: its column is the window less its gutters, so the window's width is the column's and a media query stands in. A test ties the 64rem to `SPREAD_QUERY`. It is a deliberate use of the window's width where the brief says to follow the column's: the only container that could stand there is on `PageContainer`, which is not mine.
- **Other users** (the report's sections, quotes, the abstract, the claim lists) are the same utility in the same column, so they follow the same rule. Probe (`h/align-probe.mjs`) of every visible `.reading-justify` on both tabs: at 1024 and 2560 wide all 4 on the Paper digestion tab and all 10 on the Summary tab are `left` and inside the container (none outside it); at 390, 800 and 1023 wide all of them are `left` (no shell, no container, the media rule).
- **Kept:** `hyphens: auto` and `text-wrap: pretty` in both branches (they sit on the utility's own declarations and the nested rules override only `text-align`). Compiled: `@container reader-centre not (min-width:38.5em){…}` and `@media not all and (min-width:64rem){…}` (Lightning CSS lowers the range syntax; checked in the dev sheet and in the production chunk).
- **Browsers without container queries** ignore the first rule and keep today's justification in the centre; the media rule still holds.

## Unsettled

1. **S8 is now in force in no column the app draws.** The owner asked for justified body text (S8); the brief's bar (2x on every non-final line) cannot be met by Chromium's justification of long-word technical prose at any width in this browser, so every column is ragged and the justified branch is kept only for a column wider than the shell's cap (none). This is the measured consequence of the rule as written, not a choice made here. If the owner's S8 should stand at the default 646 px, the bar has to change (a percentile bar instead of "every line"; the per-width counts and worst gaps are in the tables above), or the line breaking has to improve (item 2). One number moves it: `38.5em` in `@utility reading-justify`.
2. **Hyphenation.** The harness's Chromium has no dictionary, so `hyphens: auto` is inert there (the `lang` is fine). A real Chrome with the dictionary was not available. If it is assumed present, the justified branch should drop `text-wrap: pretty` (upper-bound runs above: 11 of 30 lines over 2x at 560 with `pretty`, 0 with `wrap`; 5 and 0 at 646) and at 646 the upper bound meets the bar on both papers; at 416 it still would not (1 to 5 of 30 lines over). Not done: the brief keeps `pretty`, and the harness cannot confirm a dictionary.
3. **The one-column page** is switched by the window's width (a media query), not a container, because the only place for a container there is `PageContainer` (not mine). Below 64rem the window and the column agree to within the gutters.
4. **Redundant, harmless, untouched:** the Ruling 13 `ScrambleText` rule (a revealing paragraph is `left`) and `explain-box.tsx`'s `[&_.reading-justify]:text-left` now say what the utility says; they would matter again if a column ever justified. Their comments still read true.
5. **The A / A step** is not part of the switch (the width is in the container's own `em`; larger type makes the gaps worse, not better, so ragged-below-the-cap holds at every step). Not measured at the larger steps.
6. **Sample:** two invented papers, twelve paragraphs each; at columns above about 1,000 px the first 30 lines are fewer than 30, so the wide end of the sweep is thin.
