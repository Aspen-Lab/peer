## 2. As built

**2a — the fixed zone holds only what must stay in view.** In the three panes (from 1024 css px) the fixed zone is the title (clamped as now: 4 lines, 3 under 700 px tall), the commands, the strip and the progress. The venue line and its age, the byline with "and N more", the affiliation — and the recommendation line (the "why it is here", not named in the ruling, but it is bibliographic and the zone must hold only the four things) — are `Bibliography` (`title-block.tsx`), drawn by `decision-block.tsx` as the head of the details, in the scroll zone after the map and the plate, whole at every height. `TitleBlock` in the pane is the `h1` alone; `AuthorLine` is back to its base markup; one column is byte for byte (pinned against the literal markup). `page.tsx`: one hunk on the details (`paper`, `recommendation`, `now`). The one-line clamp rules of the first round (hooks, `title=`, the hidden copy) are removed: **retired with their function** (named in `60badcde`): the nine `left-pane.byline.test.tsx` cases for the clamp. **Re-aimed:** `title-block.test.tsx` "takes the pane's tighter top…"; `left-pane.fixed-zone.test.tsx` "changes nothing else on a short window…" (back to what it asserted before the clamp rules).

**2b — acceptance, by the three means in the order the ruling gave.** (1) The bibliographic lines (2a). (2) *The Upload full article PDF row moves to the details*, after the bibliography and before the sentence, **in the three panes only**: one `uploadAction` element, made once in `page.tsx` (gated on `uploadsAvailable` where it is made) and handed to the commands and to the details; `Commands` draws it only below 1024, `Details` only from it; same element, same words. (3) *Compact command rows*, **under 800 css px high**, for the stack of a paper with an Open row (`:has(> a)`): each row 32 px high (hit area ≥ 32 px; touch devices keep their 44 px minimum), 4 px apart, not 40 and 8; **under 700 css px high** that stack's zone gaps (`--pane-gap`: the commands', the progress's, the strip's) are 4 px, not 8. I chose 800 because at 1280 × 720 with a report running the four-line-title paper needed it (the bar's state is 389 with 40 px rows against a cap of 379; 353 with 32 px rows), and a briefing paper's four-line title does not fit with 40 px rows below about 735 px high; 800 covers the acceptance windows with room (at 1366 × 768 the rows would fit as they were, with 20 px to spare, and are compact all the same). An upload's page has no Open row and is not touched: its first map row is **498.7** at 1280, 1366, 1440 and 1920 before and after (and 459.5 at 1024 × 600 and 1093 × 614). Every command stays (Read it here, Open the PDF, Save / Skip / Copy), the strip and the progress stay, the cap is not raised. `reader-layout.tsx`: `data-pane-commands` on the commands' wrapper, the hook the rules read.

**3b — the phone.** For the new arrivals `landAgain` (`sticky-note.tsx`): after the images on the page have loaded or failed (the plate goes when its picture fails) and two frames in which the scroll has not moved, once it has moved at all (§ 1b below), the block is scrolled to again, at once, if it is not within 2 px of 80: one extra correction, no later than 2 s after the first scroll, none if the reader moved the page themselves (wheel, touch, key, press).

**1b — an arrival lands as soon as its target is drawn.** `followHash` is told when the page's tree changes (`ArrivalEnv.onTreeChange`, a `MutationObserver` on the document) and looks then, and once as the page is mounted; the poll (250 ms × 40) stays as the outer bound and is dropped with the watching. The arrival is a **layout effect**: as a passive effect it ran 250–450 ms after the target was in the page, the time the browser took to lay out and paint the commit that drew it. Two consequences, both measured: (a) landing that early exposes a **paragraph** to the page's late settling as a block is — at 390 × 844 it ended at top −117 (the plate's 197 px collapsing under the smooth scroll), where it had landed at 80.0 a quarter second later — so `landAgain` now serves a paragraph too (the first round's "a paragraph's arrival stays as it is" cannot hold with 1b; see §7.1); (b) a smooth scroll starts a frame or more late on a long page, so two frames of an unmoved scroll are not "stopped" until it has moved (one run ended 4 px off before this).
