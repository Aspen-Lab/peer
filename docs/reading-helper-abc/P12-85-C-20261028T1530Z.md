## 2. The rule as built

`echoOf` (the fallback for a paper whose reading has no abstract; the abstract branch above it is untouched) now picks the paragraph of the setup part by words, counted as whitespace-separated tokens (`trim().split(/\s+/)`, the count `closingPartition`'s outlook rule uses):

1. scanning from the part's end, the **last paragraph of at least `ECHO_MIN_WORDS` = 40 words**;
2. if no paragraph is that long, the **longest** one; of two equals, the **later** (the strict `>` while scanning backwards).

The setup part itself (the last introduction row before the divided row that holds a paragraph, else the last row of role setup) is unchanged, and so is everything in `closingPartition`: the abstract, the loop, the 20 % share, the outlook fallback. With one paragraph in the part, or with a last paragraph that is already long, the echo is what it was.

## 3. The tests

New block in `reading-partition.echo.test.ts` (10 tests; `describe` "the echo is the setup part's last paragraph of at least 40 words, not a fragment of figure text (C P12-85)"):

- **(a) splits a paper whose setup part ends in five short fragments after a 150-word announcement.** The brief's fixture 1: it asserts the 150-word count, that the five fragments (under 10 words each) reach the part's body, that the front row is the setup row, and the split (`results 0–2`, `conclusion 3–4`), for no abstract, `null` and `""`.
- **(a) shows what the last fragment alone echoed:** the same paper handed its last fragment as the abstract is left whole (the pre-rule echo), and without it splits.
- **(a) steps back over a run of fragments of any size under 40 words:** one fragment, 15 fragments, a 39-word fragment.
- **(b) takes the last paragraph of 40 words, not the longest,** both ways about.
- **holds the paragraph to 40 words:** a 40-word announcement is the echo, a 39-word one is not (45 words of something else stands before it).
- **(c) leaves a setup part whose last paragraph is already long as it was** (the long last paragraph is the echo; an earlier long one is not).
- **(d) a setup part with no paragraph of 40 words:** the longest is the echo, wherever it stands; two equals, the later.
- **(d) a setup part of one paragraph, long or short, is that paragraph.**
- **(e) leaves a paper with an abstract as it was:** the abstract is the echo and the setup part, fragments or not, is not looked at.
- **reads a named introduction the same way** ("1 Introduction" ending in a long paragraph and fragments).

**Re-aimed test: one.** `takes the LAST paragraph of the setup part, not another` (in the P12-63 block, same name). Its input was a 16-word announcement and a 9-word unrelated paragraph; with no paragraph of 40 words the rule's second clause (the brief's "if no paragraph is that long, the longest") makes the announcement the echo wherever it stands, so the old `toBeNull()` no longer holds. The two paragraphs are now padded to 40 words with `ofWords` (the filler has no word of seven letters), which keeps what the test says: of the paragraphs that are long, the last is the echo. The second assertion (the announcement last) is unchanged. The short pair is read in the new block's (d) tests. No other test was edited, deleted, skipped or weakened; the 145 other tests of the two files are untouched.

## 6. The measures (invented input; a throwaway test printed them and was deleted, `git status` clean)

The closing's long words (7+ letters): five (together, exposure, density, predation, thickens). Setup part: two short paragraphs, a 150-word announcement (four of the five long words), then five short fragments (measured on commit 1's fixture, 1, 1, 3, 4, 4 words; commit 2 swaps the fourth for a 3-word text fragment, and the last, "Counts at sheltered shores", which is the echo before the rule, is the same: one long word, "sheltered").

- **Before the rule:** the echo is the last fragment, the closing's share of it is **0 / 5** (< 0.2), no split.
- **After:** the echo is the 150-word paragraph, the share is **4 / 5** (0.8), the paper splits (results `0–2`, conclusion `3–4`).
- **Negatives, before = after:** a paper with an abstract; a setup part whose last paragraph is already long; a setup part of one paragraph (all green on the unchanged source, as the "unchanged" cases should be). **Changes by design:** a part with no paragraph of 40 words now echoes its longest paragraph, not its last (before: the last).

## 7. For the manager

1. **The tie rule is mine, not the brief's.** "The longest one" leaves two equals open; I took the **later**, which is today's behaviour when all are equal, and which two existing tests need (an 11-word paragraph before an 11-word one in "holds the closing to the 20 % share"). It is pinned by a test and a mutation.
2. **Words are whitespace tokens**, so "Fig. 1" is two words. A caption tail of 40 or more tokens at the end of a setup part would be taken as the echo, as the last paragraph was before; C P12-82's whole captions are cut by its K1 rule, so they should not stand there.
3. **The seven-fragment shape cannot be built small.** On a small invented paper whose front text holds 10 paragraphs and more than half of the words, `reading-roles`' unheaded-trunk rule (`TRUNK`: at least 8 paragraphs and at least half of the words) makes that row the trunk, role `body`, so there is no setup part, no echo and no split, **with or without this rule**. That is the fixture's size, not the echo (a real paper's front text is a minority of its words; the five-fragment fixture is the brief's own, and a 15-fragment run in the tests splits). If a real paper's front-text row ever is the majority, the model partition owns it.
4. **`closingPartition`'s doc comment** still reads "where the paper prints none, the last paragraph of its setup part (`echoOf`)". It is outside the words the brief lets me touch; one phrase ("the last paragraph of 40 words or more of its setup part") would bring it in line.
5. **A digits-only fragment never reaches the body** (`readableSections` drops "0 10 20 30"); my first fixture counted one, commit 2 fixes it. It has no bearing on the rule.
6. **The effect on a real paper is unmeasured** (no owner PDF read). The P12-82 re-score on the Nature-style paper is what settles it; the rule's two numbers are `ECHO_MIN_WORDS` (one constant) and the tie.
7. No overlap with C P12-84 (`pdf-engine.ts`): my files are `reading-partition.ts`, its echo test and this checkpoint.
