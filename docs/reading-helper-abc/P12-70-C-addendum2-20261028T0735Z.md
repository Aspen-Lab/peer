## 9.2 The witness

`capIsWord(letters, word, vocab)`: a one-letter cap is a word of its own when the bare letter is a word of the paper (`vocabularyOf`) and the joined word is not. `placeDropCaps` reads the paper's words once, lazily, from the lines with each cap's own letters taken off (they would witness themselves), only when a one-letter cap needs them. Two places join a cap, and both ask: the cap moved to the paragraph's first line (the join is written there), and the cap that opens its own row, which `lineOf` joined by geometry (`cap.joined`; `placeDropCaps` writes the space back when the paper says the cap is a word). Otherwise the cap starts the word as before. No dependency, no dictionary. Only a one-letter cap is asked (a two-letter cap is not "a whole word" by the ruling).

Tests (inside the item-8 describe, `a one-letter cap that is a whole word is not joined to the word beside it`), each for the cap on the third row and on the first: `keeps the space when the bare word is printed in the paper and the joined word is printed nowhere` ("A" + "study", "I" + "think"; "A study" / "I think"); `joins when the joined word is printed elsewhere, though the bare word is printed too` ("A" + "lmost", "almost" printed in the paragraph: "Almost"); `joins as it always did when the bare letter is no word of the paper` ("Q" + "uillwort").
