## The rule as built

**Highlight is offered on a Summary block and on an answer, beside Explain this and Take notes, with and without a key — the margin's button, `h`, the phone's thumb-bar cell — exactly as on a body paragraph.** `canHighlight` is the one gate all three go through; it now takes a selection that names a block of the page by the id the selection reports for it (`paragraphId`: `summary-…`, or `answers-block`; a paper with no body shows its answers inside the Summary's container, where the source says "summary" and the id is still the answers'). A selection reported under the bare kind (a block with no id) is not offered it: there is no element to read the words of. The page's one `canHighlight(explainTarget)` call is untouched (`page.tsx` is not changed).

**The block's words.** The page draws a Summary block's words from many components (the lead claim, the abstract, the report's blocks, the claim lists, the answers) and the answers' block is one element for every answer, so a block's words are what its element holds, read as a browser reads a selection (`readBlockText`, `lib/notes/anchor-blocks.ts`): text nodes in order, each run of white space one space, one space where a block-level element begins or ends (`display` read from the computed style in the browser), a line break a space, nothing for scripts, styles, hidden elements and superscripts, a drawn formula one `\u0000`, and a text node's words **as its style draws them** — the Summary's small labels are set in capitals and Chrome's `selection.toString()` reads capitals ("HOW IT WAS" for the markup's "How it was"; measured: the first version made nothing for a selection in a label, silently). The same reading gives the highlight its range when it is made and finds its words when its mark is drawn. `highlightSelection` reads the block (by `document.getElementById(paragraphId)`), takes the offset the selection reader gave or, for a block of the page (which the reader gives none for), where the **live selection** began in the block's words (`selectionStartIn`, from `Range.comparePoint`; ignored when the live selection is no longer the target's words), and calls `addHighlight` with the block's id and words. The occurrence the reader selected is the one marked: measured, "was" at three places of one block marks the third when the third is selected. Where the block is not on the page, nothing is made, no note is made for it, the selection is left, no strip is raised.

**The mark.** `components/notes/block-marks.ts` (new). `BlockMarks`, mounted inside `SummaryBlock` (every block the page wraps: What is new, the proposal, the method, the results, For you, the glance, related, caveats, next step, the record, the library; the lead claim; the abstract), in `Deck` (the skim line), in the TL;DR block and in the answers' section (`for-your-questions.tsx`), renders nothing — every block's markup is what it was — and keeps the marks of the paper's highlights anchored to its id true to the note and to the block (`watchBlock`): each highlight finds its words in the block's words (`findInBlock`: where it stored them while the block still holds them there, else by its quote, nearest the old place, else nowhere — **never on other words**) and they are wrapped in `<mark data-highlight="<id>">`, the element the paper's body draws, its fill the highlight's colour and its words black or white by the higher contrast (`highlightPaint`), a highlight inside another a mark inside a mark in the deeper shade. So the colour strip's host (`markLine`/`markBox`), the square's flash (`highlightMarks`) and the strip's click all find it as they find a body mark, with no change to them. A click on a mark raises the strip (`markClickOf`, as `highlightClickOf` does in the body; a link inside a mark keeps its click; the end of a drag is not a click). `goToPassage` scrolls a page block's highlight to its first mark, centred (an answers' block can be tall), and flashes the marks; a paragraph's goes as it did.

**React owns the block's text nodes.** The painter never moves one: a text node is split (`splitText`) and only the new pieces go into a mark, so React's node stays where React put it; before every paint the block is made whole again (`unpaintBlock`), and the block is watched by a `MutationObserver` — only while it has a highlight to draw, so a block with none costs the page nothing — and drawn again from the words as they are when React writes new words into a node, removes one, or draws the block later (the answers' panel is closed on every visit: its mark is drawn when it is opened; a Summary before its report is drawn: when it is drawn). A change that leaves the block's words as they were (the class the square's flash puts on a mark) draws nothing again — it would take away the flash. A highlight made, a colour changed from the strip or from the notes window, a highlight removed repaints **in the same turn** (the strip raised right after finds its mark). A highlight found elsewhere than the note stored it is repaired in the note (`repairAnchors`, once, in a microtask), so the next load needs no search and pressing Highlight on the same words again finds its twin.

**What the notes keep.** `resolveAnchors` leaves a page block's anchors alone: no search of the paper's body, never moved into a paragraph that happens to quote the same words (the Summary's abstract sentence), never called lost. `passageIsLost` (the notes window's and the notes page's row) never calls a page block's passage lost: the row keeps its square. `note-tags.tsx` re-exports `SUMMARY_ID_PREFIX` and `ANSWERS_BLOCK_ID` (now defined with the text model, which the store shares, and tested equal); `useBlockPaper` (zustand, memory only) is told the paper on show by `registerSummaryHost`, which the page already calls with `paper.id`. The notes row, the `.md`/`.txt`/`.doc`/`.pdf` exports (they read `readHighlight`) and the square from the notes page (`#summary-…`, `#answers-block`; P11-20's arrival) are the highlight's own and unchanged.

**The paper's body is byte for byte what it was**: `paper-body.tsx` is not touched; measured on the base and on the tip, a paragraph's highlight is identical (below).

### Why a painter from outside, not marks drawn by each component (for the manager)

The brief lists `paper-words.tsx` and `for-your-questions.tsx` as "drawing the marks". Those two files draw only the lead claim, the abstract, the deck, the TL;DR block and the answers; the report's blocks (What is new, the proposal, the method, the results, For you, caveats, next step, …) are drawn by `ClaimList`, `ProposalBlock`, `ResultsBlock`, `QuoteList`, … which are not mine and which the page hands to `SummaryBlock` as opaque children. A mark drawn by React in two files would leave the rest of the Summary — most of it — without one. `SummaryBlock` (mine) is the one place that holds every block, so the painter hangs there, and on the answers' section; each of those files gains one line. The cost is that the marks are real DOM the framework does not know of; the painter's rules above (never move a React node, put the words back before every paint, watch the block) are how it stays out of React's way, and each is tested (a node's value written under a mark; a node removed; a fresh node of the same words) and was checked in the browser (`m5`: a text node's value written, a node removed, under a painted mark: the words come out whole, one mark where the words still stand, none where they do not, no error).

### What I could not measure (honestly)

- **`summary-abstract`, `summary-skim`, `summary-claim`, `summary-tldr`.** The invented PDF has no abstract, so the page draws none of the four. They share `SummaryBlock`/`Deck`/the TL;DR block's mount, which `page-blocks.marks.test.tsx` pins, and the painter is the same code; they were not drawn on the real page. The abstract is conditionally mounted by `AbstractToggle` (closed on every visit) — the same case as the answers' closed panel, which was measured. `ScrambleText` (a report arriving) changes the deck's text each frame; the painter draws again from the words on each change and finds none while they are scrambled — not measured.
- **An answer's cell without a key.** A question cannot be asked without one; the no-key cells have the Summary only. `canHighlight` is pure and the page pins it ungated by the key (`page.highlight.test.ts`); the answers' no-key buttons are therefore Take notes and Highlight by the same gate, but I did not see them.
- **Exports other than `.md`.** `.txt`, `.doc`, `.pdf` and "Copy" read the same `readHighlight` words (P11-08); only `.md` was downloaded.
- **Text a selection omits that the markup holds** other than a superscript's (`user-select: none` text in the middle of a selection; soft hyphens): not met on the page; the model would then not find the passage and the highlight is not made (nothing is written, the selection is left), never a wrong mark.
