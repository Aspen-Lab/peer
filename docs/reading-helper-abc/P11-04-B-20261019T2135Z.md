## 1. The answers in brief

1. **Model.** A block of its own: `type: "highlight"`, empty `text`, and a new field `highlight` (paper id, paragraph id, the whole passage as `quote`, `start`/`end`, a colour id). Not a sticky with an empty body: the sticky machinery removes empty stickies, numbers them, opens windows for them (§2.1). Unnumbered, no tag in the paper. In the notes window and on `/notes/<id>` it is a row of the one `NoteEditor` (like the divider and the paper card rows), showing its words on its colour. **Exports: recommend the words are exported** (the owner's decision D1; P10 7.3 was said of a sticky's context, a highlight has no other content). Removed from its strip, its row's ×, or the block menu's Delete. Survives P10-35's re-anchoring through the same `resolveAnchors` with two one-line widenings. **No store version bump, no migration** (additive; §2.7).
2. **Drawing.** A `<mark data-highlight="<block id>">` over its words with inline `background` and `color`; no tag. A mark at depth 0 wears its fill, nested at depth 1 or deeper its "deep" shade (the fill moved 14% toward its ink); every mark inside a highlight sets its own ink. Sticky marks keep their classes byte for byte where no highlight is around. The flash (P10-46) outlines the highlight's marks the same way (§3).
3. **Colour.** Six named presets (Yellow default, Green, Blue, Pink, Orange, Ink); the words' colour is the better of `#000000` / `#ffffff` by WCAG, and that choice **cannot fall under 4.58:1 for any colour** (measured over sRGB), the presets all reach 8.7:1 or more (table in §4). Same fill in light and dark. No custom colour in the first version (D3).
4. **Button.** `onHighlight(target)` writes the highlight into the paper's note (making the note if needed), draws it, clears the browser's selection, and raises one small strip in the margin at the mark's first line: six swatches and a remove button. The same strip is raised by clicking a mark. No window, not a stacked card.
5. **C split.** Four Cs on disjoint files (§6): C1 store and model, C2 the drawing and the selection offset, C3 colours, strip and the `onHighlight` hook, C4 the notes-window row and the exports. One shared file, `app/papers/[id]/page.tsx`, as two named hunks.

One defect found on the way that the highlight needs fixed: a passage that occurs twice in its paragraph is anchored to its **first** occurrence whichever one was selected (`locatePassage`, `store/notes.ts:39-47`, called without `from` at `:370`). For a sticky that was a quirk; for a highlight of a short common phrase it would light the wrong words. The selection must carry its offset (§2.8).

## 2. The model (question 1)

### 2.1 Why not "a sticky with an empty body and a colour"

Every one of these treats an empty sticky as garbage or as a numbered note:

- `withoutSticky` (`store/notes.ts:224-229`) and `removeEmptySticky` (`:395-403`) take out a sticky whose text blocks are all empty; `closeSticky` (`sticky-note.tsx:202-206`) and `removeEmptyStickies` (`:191-199`, run on leaving the page and on `pagehide`) call them. `removedEmpty` (`:236`) re-removes it from a stale editor's save. A highlight in a sticky's shape would vanish the first time its window closed.
- `addSticky` numbers by `max(anchor.n)` and `stickyCount` (`:366-367`); a highlight in that shape would use up a "Note N" and leave a heading with nothing under it in the report.
- `stickySpan` (`lib/notes/blocks.ts:258-264`), `anchorMap` (`:303-311`), `repairAnchors` (`store/notes.ts:283`), `holdsItsType` (`note-editor.tsx:470`), `duplicateBlock` (`:477`), `repairAnchored` (`:487`), `stackRows` (`lib/reader/windows.ts:167`) all read "an `h3` with an `anchor`" as a sticky. A second meaning of that shape would have to be teased apart at every one of them.

A block of its own with its own field (not the sticky's `anchor`) leaves all of those untouched: `anchor` still means "sticky", so `addSticky`, `stickySpan`, `holdsItsType`, `repairAnchored`, `stackRows` need no change.

### 2.2 The shape

```ts
// lib/notes/types.ts   (C1)
export type BlockType = /* …as now… */ | "highlight";
export type HighlightColourId = "yellow" | "green" | "blue" | "pink" | "orange" | "ink";

export interface HighlightSpot {
  paperId: string;
  /** `paper-section-<i>-p<j>`, as `paragraphAnchor` writes it. */
  paragraphId: string;
  /** The whole selected passage, whitespace collapsed (at most 1,200 chars, the selection's own cap). NOT a sticky's 300-char head. */
  quote: string;
  /** Offsets of the passage in the paragraph's text as the body renders it. Always present: a highlight with no range is not made. */
  start: number;
  end: number;
  colour: HighlightColourId;
}
export interface Block { /* …as now… */ highlight?: HighlightSpot; }   // only on type "highlight"; `text` is ""
```

- `isTextual("highlight")` is false (`blocks.ts:12-14`), exactly as for `divider` and `paper`: no caret, no text, no turn-into, skipped by `citedKeys`, `excerpt`, `wordCount`; `isUntouched` (`:212-217`) is false for a note holding one, so "Write" never reuses or discards it.
- The full passage is kept as `quote` (a sticky keeps 300 characters because its words are context; a highlight's words are its content and its mark is drawn over all of them). `anchorRange` (`store/notes.ts:58-71`) checks the words at `[start, end)` *begin with* the quote; with the whole passage as the quote the check is exact, so a paper that changed inside a highlight draws no mark rather than a mark over other words. `findQuote` (`:144-174`) then searches for the whole passage. Cost: at most 1,200 characters per highlight in `localStorage` (typical 60-200); 300 highlights of the longest kind would be about 360 KB of the browser's roughly 5 MB, shared with `peer-reading-v2`.
- Made only for a selection in the paper's body (`target.source` undefined or `"paper"`). A Summary or answer selection has no paragraph to mark; the button is not offered there (`canHighlight(target)`, C3). Made only when `locatePassage` finds the passage whole; otherwise nothing is made (a sticky tolerates a range-less anchor because its tag stands at the paragraph's end; a highlight has no tag, so a range-less one would be invisible).
- A selection that is exactly the range of an existing highlight in the same paragraph does not make a second block: the existing highlight's strip is raised instead (no duplicates; it is how the reader "presses Highlight again to change the colour").

### 2.3 Numbering

Apart: highlights have no number and no `[n]` tag. A highlighter is high-volume (dozens per paper); a tag after each would clutter the paragraph, and "Note 7" that is only a coloured passage would sit in the report with no words of the reader's under it. Identity is the block id (`data-highlight`). Recommended; D2.

### 2.4 In the notes window and on `/notes/<id>`

Both surfaces render the paper's note through the same `NoteEditor` (`notes-window.tsx:329`, `app/notes/[id]/page.tsx:31`), so one row component serves both. `BlockRow` already has non-textual rows for `divider` (`note-editor.tsx:283`) and `paper` (`:289`); `TYPE_LABEL: Record<BlockType, string>` (`:155`) makes the compiler list every place that must learn the new type.

The row (mock: `shots/P11-04-D-notes-window-mock-light.png`, injected into the real window beside two real stickies): at the left a 14 px square in the highlight's colour, the same size and place as the sticky's blue square, and doing the same thing (`aria-label="Go to the passage"`: scroll to the paragraph and flash the marks); then the words on their colour, two lines at most, cut with an ellipsis (`quoteHead`, `blocks.ts:241`); at the right a colour button (its name) that opens the same swatches as the strip, and a ×. The gutter's "+" and grip stay (the reader can move the row; Duplicate and "turn into" are not offered). A lost passage (the paper on show does not hold the words) shows `PASSAGE_LOST_LINE` ("This passage is not in this version of the paper.") and no go-to square, the P10-36 behaviour, keyed by the block id through `useLostPassages`.

### 2.5 In the exports (the owner's decision D1)

P10 7.3: "引用的文字就算了不要导出" (as for the quoted words, don't export them). `export.ts:3-7` and `:63`/`:191`/`:289` implement it: a sticky's quote is on its heading's anchor and no block's text; `quote` blocks leave no line. A highlight's quote is its whole content. If it follows 7.3 to the letter, a note made of highlights exports as an empty report while the notes window shows them.

**Recommended default: export the words.** Per format:

| Format | A highlight leaves as |
|---|---|
| Markdown | its own paragraph, `==words==` (the notes' own highlight syntax, `inline.ts:54`, Obsidian's and Pandoc's) |
| Plain text | its own paragraph, the words |
| Word / PDF | `<p><mark style="background:<fill>;color:<ink>">words</mark></p>`; `mso-highlight:yellow` added only for Yellow (Word's highlighter has a fixed palette; the existing `HIGHLIGHT` constant, `export.ts:249`, already does this for the inline mark). The print stylesheet already forces colours (`print-color-adjust: exact`, `:386`). Not checked in Word here. |

Alternative B (strict 7.3): highlights export as nothing. One line to change in each of the three builders (`blockMarkdown` `:61`, `blockText` `:190`, `bodyHtml` `:279`); the same `case "highlight"` returns `null` in each. C4 builds A behind one constant (`EXPORT_HIGHLIGHT_WORDS = true`) so the owner's answer is a one-word change.

### 2.6 Removal

1. The strip's remove button (raised after a highlight and on a click of a mark). 2. The notes row's ×. 3. The block menu's Delete (`blockAction`, `note-editor.tsx:1622`, which has its own undo). All three call `removeHighlight(noteId, blockId)`; the mark leaves the paragraph in the same render. Deleting the paper's note deletes its highlights (they are blocks of it, as stickies are).

### 2.7 Re-anchoring, the persisted version, the migration

- `resolveAnchors` (`store/notes.ts:98-131`) is generic over `NoteAnchorMap` spots (`{id, n, start, end, quote}`): it checks `anchorHolds`, else `findQuote`, and returns `map` / `fixes` / `lost`, copying any extra fields of a spot (`put(there, {...spot, ...})`). So highlights ride the one map: `anchorMap` (C1) also emits a spot per highlight block, `{ id: block.id, n: 0, start, end, quote, colour, order: <index in blocks> }`, and `PaperBody` (`paper-body.tsx:917-922`) and `page.tsx:931` need no change. Two one-line widenings: `repairAnchors` (`:283`) also matches `b.type === "highlight" && b.highlight` and writes back into `highlight` (not `anchor`); `anchorMap`'s filter (`blocks.ts:306`).
- `useLostPassages` (`:303`) is keyed by the heading id; keyed by the highlight's block id it works as is.
- **No bump of `NOTES_STORE_VERSION` (`:323`), no migration.** The change is additive: an optional field and a new block type; every stored v2 note loads as it is, `migrateNotes` (`:326-331`) only concerns `< 2`. A bump would run a no-op migration in every reader's browser for nothing. `notes.migrate.test.ts` ("leaves a store already at the current version as it is") stays as it is. What stored data needs instead is a **sanitiser on read** (a pure `readHighlight(block)` in `blocks.ts`): an unknown `colour` reads as `"yellow"`; a block with `type: "highlight"` whose `highlight` is missing or malformed is shown as a row without words and drawn nowhere, never an exception. (A future preset removal then needs no migration either.)

### 2.8 The selection must carry its offset (a defect fix the highlight depends on)

`addSticky` calls `locatePassage(paragraphText, passage)` with no `from`, so the passage is found at its first occurrence (`store/notes.ts:39-47`, `:370`). With "tide pools" twice in a paragraph, selecting the second lights the first. `readSelection` (`paper-body.tsx:509-531`) has the range but returns only the paragraph id and the words. Specification: `SelectionTarget` gets an optional `offset?: number` (the length of the paragraph's words before the selection's start: a `Range` from the `<p>`'s start to the selection's start, `cloneContents()`, `wordsWithoutTags()` at `paper-body.tsx:489`, else `toString()`); `locatePassage` gets an optional hint and returns the occurrence whose `start` is nearest to it. In a paragraph with a formula the DOM text and the raw text differ (KaTeX renders its own nodes), so the hint is only a hint — nearest occurrence, never required. `addSticky` may take the same hint (C1 passes it through; whether the sticky gets it is the manager's call, it is the same bug there).

## 3. The drawing (question 2)

### 3.1 What a highlight's mark is

In `notedRuns` (`paper-body.tsx:772-793`) a spot with a `colour` draws

```tsx
<mark data-highlight={spot.id} style={{ backgroundColor: paint.bg, color: paint.ink }} onClick={…}>…</mark>
```

with no `NoteTag` after it (the sticky branch at `:784-791` is the only one that draws a tag; a `tag` segment of a highlight spot, which `passageSegments` makes for a range the text no longer holds, `note-tags.tsx:105`, and for a fully covered one, `:93-95`, is dropped). `paint` comes from C3's `highlightPaint(colour, depth)`: depth 0 the fill, depth 1 or deeper the deep shade; ink the better of black/white for the colour actually painted. The reader's `<mark>` default styles (browser yellow, black) are overridden by the inline style.

The sticky branch is **not touched when no highlight is around**: `MARK_OUTER` / `MARK_INNER` (`:768-769`) stay as they are, because `paper-body.mark.test.tsx` asserts those class names (lines 96-98, 140, 308-309, 368, 386-387) and `globals.css.test.ts:299` allow-lists exactly `components/reader/paper-body.tsx: bg-[color:var(--color-note-mark)]` as the only reader of the token. The highlight colours are a TypeScript table (§4), not CSS tokens; the token tests do not see them.

### 3.2 What changes where a highlight meets a sticky's mark or another highlight

`passageSegments` (`note-tags.tsx:75-107`) and `coveringSegments` (`paper-body.tsx:754-765`) cut the geometry; they do not care what a spot is, so highlights take part in the same tree without change to the cutting. Three small things change:

1. **The order of a tie.** `passageSegments` sorts "by start, longer first, then the earlier note (`a.spot.n - b.spot.n`)" (`:84`) and `coveringSegments` likewise (`:759`). Highlight spots have `n: 0`; the tie-break becomes `(kind rank, order ?? n)`: on equal ranges a sticky is the outer, between two highlights the earlier block. Paragraphs with stickies only are sorted exactly as before.
2. **Which paint a nested mark wears** (mock, both themes: `shots/P11-04-B-proposed-marks-light.png`, `shots/P11-04-B-proposed-marks-dark.png`; paragraph 3 to 5 are the cases):

| Case | What is drawn |
|---|---|
| A highlight alone | its fill, its ink |
| A highlight inside a sticky's mark (p.3, "team counted") | the highlight at depth 1: its deep shade, its ink; the sticky's mark around it unchanged |
| A highlight inside a highlight, same colour (p.4, "team counted 14") | the inner at its deep shade (olive for Yellow), 1.37:1 against the fill: visible, a clearer step than P10-46's sticky pair (1.097:1) |
| A highlight inside another of another colour (p.4, blue in pink) | the inner's own deep shade |
| Partial overlap of two highlights (p.5, yellow then green) | `passageSegments` clips the later to start where the earlier ends and `coveringSegments` lays its lost words back as a nested `part` mark: the overlap in the later one's deep shade, the rest of it in its fill. In general the smaller range sits on top |
| A sticky's mark inside a highlight (p.3, "visited twice") | **needs a rule**: today it would wear `--color-note-nested` and **inherit** the highlight's ink. Inherited black on the dark theme's nested token is 1.59:1 (unreadable). The mock gave it an explicit muted ink: legible (5.61:1) but in the dark theme the brown patch reads as a hole in the green highlight. Recommended: a sticky's mark that has a highlight among its ancestors wears that highlight's deep shade and ink (it keeps its `[n]` tag). Only paragraphs that hold a highlight are affected |

3. **The ink is explicit on every mark that can sit inside a highlight** (invariant I3). Today sticky marks use `text-inherit`, safe only because the paragraph's ink is the only ink there is.

A highlight and a sticky mark in the same family: the pale sticky mark `#fdf0a6` and the default `#ffe600` are 1.10:1 apart in lightness on the light theme (the screenshot shows them distinct by chroma and by the sticky's `[n]` tag) and 9.04:1 on the dark theme. Kept as is (D7).

### 3.3 The click, the flash

- A click on a highlight's mark with a collapsed selection raises its strip (`onClick` reads `getSelection()?.isCollapsed`); a drag that ends on a mark is a selection, not a click. The mark is not a button for a keyboard; the notes row is that path.
- The flash (`goToPassage`, `note-tags.tsx:172-200`): the selector at `:187` reads `mark[data-note="n"], mark[data-note-part="n"]`. A highlight is flashed through `mark[data-highlight="id"], mark[data-highlight-part="id"]` with the same `MARK_FLASH_CLASSES` (2 px accent outline, 2 px offset, so the outline stands on the page ground, not on the fill, and reads on every preset). The `part` marks of a highlight carry `data-highlight-part`, as the sticky's carry `data-note-part` (P10-46), so a highlight cut by `coveringSegments` flashes whole. `goToPassage` takes either an anchor or `{ paperId, paragraphId, highlightId }`.

## 4. Colour and contrast (question 3)

### 4.1 The rule, and why it cannot fail

The words' colour is whichever of `#000000` and `#ffffff` has the higher WCAG 2.x contrast against the colour painted (ties: black). The two ratios cross at relative luminance 0.179, where both are 4.58:1; so for **every** colour the better ink gives at least 4.58:1 (checked over a 52³ sample of sRGB: the worst was `#4b7d87`, 4.58). A custom colour would therefore also never fall under 4.5:1, but a mid-luminance one sits at 4.6:1 while the presets give 8.7:1 or more, so the presets are chosen away from that band. Script: `m/presets.mjs`.

### 4.2 The presets (recommended six; same fill and ink in both themes)

Measured by `m/presets.mjs` (WCAG relative luminance) and, for the computed style, in Chromium (`out/s02-*.json`: yellow `rgb(255, 230, 0)` / `rgb(0, 0, 0)` and the others as below).

| id | name | fill | ink | ink on fill | other ink | deep (fill 14% toward its ink) | ink on deep | fill vs light page `#fafafa` | fill vs dark page `#111111` | fill vs deep |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yellow (default) | Yellow | `#ffe600` | `#000000` | 16.57 | white 1.27 | `#dbc600` | 12.09 | 1.21 | 14.90 | 1.37 |
| green | Green | `#8fe388` | `#000000` | 13.51 | white 1.55 | `#7bc375` | 9.91 | 1.49 | 12.15 | 1.36 |
| blue | Blue | `#7cc4ff` | `#000000` | 11.20 | white 1.88 | `#6ba9db` | 8.32 | 1.80 | 10.07 | 1.35 |
| pink | Pink | `#ff9ec7` | `#000000` | 10.97 | white 1.91 | `#db88ab` | 8.12 | 1.83 | 9.86 | 1.35 |
| orange | Orange | `#ffb84d` | `#000000` | 12.22 | white 1.72 | `#db9e42` | 8.98 | 1.65 | 10.98 | 1.36 |
| ink | Ink | `#1e40af` | `#ffffff` | 8.72 | black 2.41 | `#3e5bba` | 6.16 | 8.36 | 2.16 | 1.42 |

- "Words' contrast in light and dark": the words and the fill are the same in both themes, so the first columns do not move; what the theme changes is how the patch stands against the page (the last columns). The bright patch on the dark page is glaring (yellow 14.9:1) and that is what a highlighter is; the owner asked for bright yellow with black words. A dimmed dark-theme variant is not recommended (D6): it would make the black words fall.
- Why one dark preset ("Ink"): the white-words branch is otherwise reachable only by a custom colour, and the owner's text says the words are "white or black". Drop it if the owner wants only light fills (five presets); nothing else changes.
- Colour-vision screening (Machado 2009 simulation, ΔE76, a screening estimate, not a test with people): the least distinct pair among the six is 39.0 normally (yellow/orange), 24.8 under deuteranopia (yellow/orange), 20.1 under protanopia (blue/pink), 11.7 under tritanopia (pink/orange). The weak pair under tritanopia is pink/orange; the colour's name is always shown (strip tooltip and aria-label, the notes row's button), so the colour is never the only carrier.
- The existing sticky tokens for comparison (`globals.css:315-322`, `:376-381`): light `#fdf0a6` with the muted ink 4.97:1, nested `#fbe48d` 4.53:1; dark `#403a14` 4.87:1, nested `#3a2f10` 5.61:1. Unchanged.

### 4.3 Custom colour

Not in the first version (D3). A native colour input is cheap and the rule above keeps any pick at 4.58:1 or more, but: an arbitrary pick can be a mid-tone with the weakest margin; the Word export's `mso-highlight` has a fixed palette (so a custom colour would only be right in other readers); and the stored value changes from a table id to a hex string that has to be validated on every read. The store already allows it later without a migration: `colour` is a table id today, a `#rrggbb` string would be a second legal form in `readHighlight`.

### 4.4 Where the colour is changed

1. The strip (§5), raised after a highlight and by a click on a mark. 2. The notes row's colour button (the same swatches, C3's component). Not the paper's mark itself beyond the click. The change is `setHighlightColour(noteId, blockId, colour)`; the mark and the row repaint from the store.

## 5. The button and the margin (question 4)

`onHighlight(target: ExplainSelection)` (P11-03 owns the button; this is what it calls, C3's `useHighlight({ paper, questions, forYourQuestions, body })`, a copy of `useTakeNote`'s shape, `sticky-note.tsx:240-259`):

1. `canHighlight(target)`: `target.source` undefined or `"paper"`; else return.
2. `ensureReadingNote(citableFromPaper(paper), { questions, forYourQuestions })` (as `takeStickyNote`, `:219-220`): the paper's note is made the first time, without navigating.
3. `addHighlight(noteId, { paperId, paragraphId: paragraphAnchor(sectionIndex, paragraphIndex), passage: stripTagText(target.passage), paragraphText: body[sectionIndex].paragraphs[paragraphIndex], hint: target.offset, colour })`, `colour` yellow. It returns `{ id, created }` or null (passage not found whole). An identical existing range returns `{ id, created: false }`.
4. `window.getSelection()?.removeAllRanges()`: the mark shows without the browser's selection over it, and `useSelectionListener` reports `null`, so the three actions leave the margin.
5. Raise the strip for `id`: `useHighlightStrip.getState().show(id)`.

It does **not** open a window, does not call `holdInPlace` (no column is reserved: the text does not reflow), and is not a stacked card (rule 7.1a is for figure, sticky and Explain; a highlight is quick, a window would be heavier than the act).

**What the margin shows after a highlight**: one small strip, in the margin, level with the mark's first line (the same slot the three actions stood in): six swatches (24 px hit area, the current one ringed, `role="radiogroup"`, each `aria-label` its name) and a remove button. It goes on Esc, on a click elsewhere, on the next selection, or when its mark is more than a screen away; never on a timer. With the margin collapsed (P11-02) there is no strip, the mark is still drawn, and the colour is changed from the notes window. A strip is at most about 214 px wide; it wraps to two rows in a narrower margin rather than overflow.

### Contracts I assume with the other items (the manager reconciles)

- **P11-03 (the three actions in the margin).** (a) Highlight is offered for every paper-body selection, **with or without a key**: in my no-key run the old pill drew "Take notes" alone (`out/s02-light.json`, `buttons: ["Take notes"]`, because `selectionCanExplain` gates Explain only, `selection-toolbar.tsx:31-34`); Highlight must not be gated by `canExplain`. (b) `onHighlight` receives the same `ExplainSelection` object the other two receive, untouched (it carries `measure`, and `offset` once §2.8 lands). (c) The margin has one slot at a given height; the three actions have it while a selection exists, the strip has it after the selection is cleared, and the strip may stand over a stacked card for as long as it is shown (z-index as the old pill's, `z-[70]`). (d) An optional key (`h`, as `e` is for Explain) is P11-03's and the owner's to decide; the foot legend has no room named for it.
- **P11-02 (panes).** The strip and the marks sit in the right pane; the centre column re-flows at the same font size, and `readSelection` / marks read the DOM, so a resize of the panes changes nothing in a highlight (offsets are text offsets, not pixels). If the right pane can be collapsed to zero, §5's fallback holds.
- **P11-01 (figure viewer in the margin).** A highlight never enters `useWindows`, so `stackRows` / `stackRoom` (`lib/reader/windows.ts`) are not affected.

## 6. The C split (question 5)

The Cs are independent once the contract of §2.2 (types) and the signatures below are fixed by the manager before they start; none imports a file another C writes except these named ones: types from C1's `types.ts` (read-only for the others), the colour table from C3's `highlight-colours.ts` (read-only for the others).

## 7. Invariants that must hold

- I1. The paragraph's text is unchanged: the runs and marks, in order, are exactly the paragraph (the existing property of `passageSegments`), with highlights included.
- I2. A highlight is drawn only on words the paragraph holds (`anchorRange`); a lost one draws nothing and its row says so.
- I3. Every `<mark>` that can sit inside a highlight sets its own ink; the ink on the painted shade (fill or deep) is at least 4.5:1, for every preset, in both themes (unit-tested over the table).
- I4. A paragraph with no highlight renders byte-identically to main (the sticky classes and tests are untouched).
- I5. Highlights never change a sticky's number (`addSticky` reads `anchor.n` only) and never open a window or enter `useWindows` / `stackRows`.
- I6. `withoutSticky`, `removeEmptySticky`, `removeEmptyStickies` never remove a highlight; `stickySpan` ends at one.
- I7. A highlight needs no key and no model (Tier 0), and sends nothing.
- I8. A stored colour that is not in the table reads as Yellow; a malformed `highlight` field never throws.
- I9. No highlight from a Summary or answer selection; none without the whole passage found in its paragraph; no duplicate of an existing identical range.
- I10. The anchor of a highlight is the occurrence the reader selected (offset hint), not the first occurrence of its words.
- I11. A highlight made in the paper while the notes window holds unsaved words survives the window's write, and the window's stale copy never removes or recolours it (`mergeBlocks`, P10-29).
- I12. A note made of highlights only is not "untouched" and is not empty in the notes window; whether it is empty in an export is D1.

## 8. What the owner must decide (each with the recommended default)

- **D1. Do the exports carry the highlights' words?** Default: yes (§2.5), as `==words==` / plain paragraph / coloured `<mark>`. It narrows P10 7.3's "quoted text is not exported" to stickies' context and `quote` blocks. If no: a highlight leaves nothing, and a highlights-only note exports empty.
- **D2. Are highlights numbered and tagged `[n]` in the paper?** Default: no (§2.3).
- **D3. The colours.** Default: six presets (Yellow, Green, Blue, Pink, Orange, Ink), no custom colour. Alternatives: five (drop Ink); a custom colour input.
- **D4. The next highlight's colour.** Default: always Yellow, the owner's default. Alternative: remember the last colour used (a real pen stays in the hand).
- **D5. A highlight across paragraphs.** Not possible today: the selection reader refuses a selection that leaves its paragraph (`readSelection`, `paper-body.tsx:520`), for stickies too. Default: one paragraph at a time; say so if it is needed (it is a larger change to the selection and the anchor).
- **D6. The dark theme.** Default: the same bright fills and black or white words in both themes. Alternative: dimmed fills in the dark (the words' contrast would then be chosen against the dimmed fill; the owner's "bright yellow" would not hold there).
- **D7. The sticky's own mark** stays the pale yellow with its `[n]` tag. Alternative: a different hue, if the pale yellow and a Yellow highlight are too alike on the light theme (1.10:1 in lightness; different by chroma and the tag).
- **D8. Highlighting words of the Summary or of an answer.** Default: not offered (they are regenerated; there is no stable paragraph to anchor to).
