## 2. The rules as built

**Rule 1 — `MattedFigure`.** It holds `failedSrc` (keyed on the picture's URL, as the lead plate's failure is, so a new picture for the same place is tried again) and hands the lightbox an `onError`. When the picture fails the whole mat goes — the `<img>`, the lightbox button, the mat, the crop marks — and what stands is `<figure class="mt-4"><figcaption class="font-reading text-body-sm text-text-muted">caption</figcaption></figure>`: the caption once, no `alt` anywhere to say it again, no broken glyph, the Reading's own `Figure` (paper-body.tsx) as the pattern. A figure with no caption that fails draws nothing at all (`null`: no empty figure, no `mt-4`). The picture that loads is untouched (first-paint markup pinned byte for byte, §3). `"use client"` was added (its three importers already are).

**Rule 2 — the lead plate.** Two parts, one in each direction:

- *The box.* A lead plate whose picture failed (`lead && failedSrc === src`) keeps the figure's box: `LEAD_LOST_BOX = tile-cover grain ${BOX_SHAPE} ${LEAD_CAP} …`, where `BOX_SHAPE` (`overflow-hidden aspect-[16/9] @container`) is now the one constant `FIGURE_BOX` is also built from, and `LEAD_CAP` (`max-xl:max-h-[240px] max-xl:w-full`) is the one-column cap, so the failed plate and the held / drawn plate have the same height by construction (their rendered class string is byte-identical to before for the picture and the held slot). Its look is the terms plate's — the card's surface and grain, the paper's terms at the foot, `data-plate="terms"`, no mat, no crop marks; a paper with no terms keeps the empty window and its rule in the same box. The feed's tile (no `lead`) still falls to the 2.4:1 plate. I chose "the held box stays the box" over a shorter terms plate: a shorter one is the step the walk measured (the h1 moving up 47 / 51 px under a reader about to press "Read it here").
- *The caption.* **The brief assumes the plate draws the caption; it does not.** The line under the lead plate is `papers/[id]/page.tsx`'s own `<figcaption>` (lines 1379-1389: `{caption && (<figcaption …>)}`, the next child of the `<figure>` that holds the `SwipeableCard`), and the page never sees the failure: it is `PaperPlate`'s own state (`failedSrc`), set by the browser's `error` event on the plate's `<img>`. So that the lead plate's caption goes with its picture without touching the page, the lost box carries the Tailwind variant `[figure:has(&)>figcaption]:hidden`, emitted (dev and production, checked in `.next/static/chunks/*.css`) as `figure:has(.\[figure…\]:hidden)>figcaption{display:none}`: a figure whose plate has lost its picture has no caption. It is on the lost lead box only (not the held slot, not the drawn picture, not a tile), and the test reads the page for the shape it needs (the plate's bare `<figure>`, the card, the `figcaption` as the card's next sibling and the figure's last child).

*Why not the callback (commit 3, replaced by commit 4).* An `onFigureError(src)` on the plate, with the page keeping a `failedLead` state and dropping its `figcaption` when `failedLead === plateImage`, is the explicit form and removes the node. It needs three lines in `papers/[id]/page.tsx` (not mine), and without them commit 3 alone left the caption under a 16:9 terms plate with the h1 under it 46.7 px lower than the head's (arithmetic from §6: 300.5 against 253.8) — a state worse than the head's. The CSS closes the rule inside my files and is measured on the real page (§6). Costs: the caption node stays in the DOM with `display: none` (a script that reads `figcaption.textContent` still finds the words; one that reads the box finds 0 px), and the coupling to the page's markup is implicit (pinned by the test, §3). §8 item 1 gives the page patch if the explicit form is preferred; both can stand, but I built one.

**Rule 3 — nothing else.** The picture when it loads, the feed's tiles, the Reading's figures: not changed. `showFigure`, the held slot, `leadFigurePending`, `isSameFigure`, `leadCaption`, and both `onError` handlers (`() => setFailedSrc(src)`) are as they were.
