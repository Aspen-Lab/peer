## 3. The rule and the means

**Rule (§1h.160):** an empty bar shows no clipped line; its placeholder is whole on its one row, or on one row cut at its end with an ellipsis; no part of a second line is visible; nothing else changes.

**Means chosen:** CSS on the textarea's own classes, one row kept (the bar's design, P10 review 2.2), no JavaScript.

`placeholder:whitespace-nowrap placeholder:overflow-hidden placeholder:text-ellipsis placeholder-shown:text-ellipsis`

What the measurement taught (Chromium 141, tried one rule at a time on the live page before any code):
1. `::placeholder { white-space: nowrap }` is honoured on a `textarea` (the UA's `pre-wrap` loses): the placeholder stays on one line, and `scrollHeight` falls to the box's height. Alone it ends the second line but leaves the last glyph cut through at the box's edge (a half-visible "?" at 1.6): not clean.
2. `text-overflow: ellipsis` on `::placeholder` alone is **ignored** (the computed value stays `clip`), as are `line-clamp` (not in this Chromium) and `-webkit-line-clamp` (the placeholder's `display` stays `block`). Chromium reads the placeholder's `text-overflow` only while the **textarea's own** is not `clip`. So the textarea says `ellipsis` as well, and the placeholder says `overflow: hidden` + `text-overflow: ellipsis`.
3. The textarea's `text-overflow` is scoped to `:placeholder-shown` (`placeholder-shown:text-ellipsis`): the same effect on the placeholder, and a typed question's textarea is given no new property at all.

Why this and not the other means the brief names: the bar growing to the placeholder's height would reverse the P10 design (and the surviving `fitHeight` test, "an empty bar is one row whatever its placeholder", would have to be re-aimed) and would make the bar 100 px tall at the largest step; a measured shortening of the placeholder in JavaScript (canvas width, `ResizeObserver`, font-load events) is machinery for a CSS property that does the same exactly, in the browser's own layout, at every step, window and scheme. `fitHeight` itself is unchanged: its empty branch still clears the height; only its comment names the means.

The words are `ASK.placeholder` and `ASK.chips.gist`, whole in the attribute (the tests that pin `placeholder="…"` and `placeholder:text-text-muted` are green, untouched); the ellipsis is drawn by the browser, not written into the copy.

## 8. Unsettled

- **Chromium only was measured.** `white-space: nowrap` and `overflow: hidden` on `::placeholder` are standard and keep the row to one line in any engine, so no second line shows in any; whether another engine also draws the ellipsis (the property Chromium reads from the textarea) was not measured: where it does not, the row is cut at its box's edge as in step 1 of §3.
- The ellipsis is the browser's: it ends the row at the last whole glyph that fits, so it can fall inside a word ("…answe…" at the worst cell), not at a word boundary. A word-boundary cut would need the placeholder shortened in script, which §3 declines.
- The long typed question is not exactly three lines in every cell (the wrap depends on the column); the 32 cells that are exactly three, and the A/B on the other widths, show a typed bar unchanged.
- The bar at 390 is at 16 px at every step by the stylesheet's iOS rule; if a later change lets the text steps scale it there, the same classes already keep the placeholder to one row.
