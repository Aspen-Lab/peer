## The symptom

B P12-40: **O1** — `bodyStyle` takes the size with the most characters; in a paper whose methods, references and captions are set small (7.5 pt) and outweigh the 9.3 pt body, every body line looks "bigger" and short lines become headings (P3: 100 sections from 11 pages; the what-if below gives 11). **O3** — `headingOf` takes any line starting with up to three digits and a capital as a numbered heading: street-address lines and reference entries become rows. **O4** — running heads are keyed by whole-line text and height, so two heads on one row in two columns are not furniture, and a running head becomes a heading. **O7** — with O1 repaired, a drop cap becomes a heading welded to the first line, and bold run-in lead-ins ("Word word." then body text on the same line) become headings holding the whole first line.

## The rules

1. **O1.** The body size is **the largest size holding at least 35 % of the heaviest size's characters** (B P12-40's measured what-if); the body font follows. A paper whose heaviest size is the body is unchanged (test it).
2. **O3.** A line is a numbered heading only if it is set apart as a heading (the existing face / size / shortness tests) **and** its number continues the paper's section numbering (a next top-level number, or a sub-number of the current one); a street-address shape (a number, capitalised words and a comma or a postal code) and any line inside a references section are never headings. Negative tests for both.
3. **O4.** A running head is furniture by its normalised text when it repeats in the top or bottom band on several pages, whatever its column or exact height; two heads on one row in two columns are both furniture. A one-off line is not.
4. **O7.** A drop cap (one or two large letters) is never a heading; it joins the word it starts. A run-in lead-in (a phrase in another face ending with a period, followed by body-face text on the same line) is not a heading; it stays at the start of its paragraph.
5. Nothing else changes: captions, columns, section assembly, the paragraph joins.
