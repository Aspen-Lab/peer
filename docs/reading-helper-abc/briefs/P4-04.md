**The rule (§1h.8 (8)).** A verified quote — one that `supportedIn` accepts as a verbatim substring of a section — is shown to its sentence boundaries: when it starts or stops inside a sentence of that section, the shown quote is extended to that sentence's start and end if the whole extended text fits the quote cap (400 characters, the same cap everywhere); otherwise the quote is shown as it is with the cut marked by an ellipsis ("…") at each end that is a cut, never silently. A quote already on its sentence boundaries is unchanged. The shown text is always the paper's own characters (the O-6 notation rule holds: `f_cell`, `α_1`, citation brackets as the paper has them), never the model's. This applies in the explain first answer, the explain reply, and the deep report's evidence alike, through ONE function in `evidence.ts` (named for what it does, for example `shapeEvidenceQuote(sectionText, quote, cap)`) that all three call; the three do not keep their own copies of the logic. Where `report.ts` has its own cleaner for the evidence, it calls the shared function and keeps only what is specific to it.

Design freedom, constraints: no new model call, no new prompt text unless a test needs it; a quote the verifier does not support is dropped as before (never extended); the ellipsis character is the one the page already uses for cut text (grep before choosing); a section's sentence splitting is `splitSentences` from `skim.ts`, not a new regex; abbreviations and decimals (`Fig. 3`, `0.4 V`, `et al.`) must not split a sentence at their dot (test them with invented text); the 400 cap binds the whole shown quote including the extension.
