### 2.1 `refusedNumbers`, as the log lines give them

C P12-90 put the numbers of the refused corrections on both lines: the `unusable` line (a fallback) and the `review` line (a used review; its first eight). Each entry: `op`, `reason` (closed-set names), then `part`, `ordinal`, the room the correction was judged against (`from`, `to`: the named part's first and last paragraph in the draft), `draftLength` (parts of the draft) and `paragraphs` (paragraphs of the paper). The role each named is one of the five tags and is left out here.

**P4 (both runs, the same line):** `cause` all-refused, `read` 1, `rejected` 1, `refusedByOp` {split: 1}, `refusedByReason` {out-of-range: 1}; `promptChars` 13,530, `answerChars` 95. One entry:

| op | reason | part | ordinal | from | to | draftLength | paragraphs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| split | out-of-range | 1 | **147** | 35 | **146** | 5 | 164 |

The part exists (1 is below 5), and the split's ordinal is one above the part's last paragraph. From reading `structure-review.ts` at this head (not a measurement): a split is refused as out-of-range when its ordinal is at or below the part's first paragraph or above its last; so this is the "a split outside the part's own room" kind, not the "a part that does not exist" kind, and it is off the bound by exactly 1. The reply is the same size in both runs and in all three phase-3 runs (95 characters, 28 output tokens), which fits the one entry above in each; phase 3 could not name it.
