## 2. The rule, as built

**Where.** `PdfLine` gains `runs` (the row's runs, left to right) and `raised` (the fold's set), which `withStackFacts` (P9-13a's helper) fills for a row `isFragment` accepts and no other. The display branch of `foldStacks` calls `writeIsland(rows, leading)` (new, exported, after `foldStacks`) and falls back to `rows.join("\n")` when it returns null; `flush()` needed no change. The `[EQUATION]` marker, the island's boundaries and P9-09's display / inline / sandwich rules, P9-13a's fraction around a text row, and the numbered-line rule (`equationOf` lifts a numbered line before any island is looked for) are untouched.

**The writer.**

1. **Cells.** Each row's runs are cut into cells wherever the next run starts more than 0.9 em of the island's largest text size past the ones before it (`CELL_GAP_EM`), and (commit 2) before a script-size run that stands more than 0.4 em clear of the visible run before it and off that run's baseline (`SCRIPT_APART_EM`): an exponent or a subscript touches its base, a numerator the fold put into a row it only half belongs to does not. A one-cell row keeps `line.text` exactly; a cell of a many-cell row is read through `lineOf` (the way P9-13a reads a head). A cell has its tallest run's baseline and height.
2. **The base line.** The text-size row (not under 0.75 of the island's largest, the fold's own `SCRIPT_RATIO`) that holds `=`; with none, the widest text-size row. Two text-size rows that hold `=` (an equation over several lines), or a block with neither a letter nor an `=` (a column of figures), return null: the rows are kept.
3. **Sides.** A cell of another row is above or below by its row; a cell of the base row is above or below when its baseline is more than the line tolerance (2.2 pt) off the base line, else it is the line's own text. So a script the fold took into the base line and left a few points under it, set apart by a gap, is stack material, not part of the line.
4. **Limit.** A script-size cell of at most 3 characters over the x-range of an operator run (`Σ ∑ ∏ ∫ lim`), under it or over it (the nearest each way, within 1.2 x leading), is that run's limit: written right after it (`Σn`), below first, then above. It runs on the line's own cells too, so `S = Σ I` over `n` is one line `S = Σn I` with no fraction at all. With no operator run in the text layer nothing is moved: `n I(hkl)` stays `n I(hkl)`.
5. **A fraction inside a side.** A script-size cell of at most 3 characters between the base line and a text-size cell of its own side, within 0.9 em of it (the nearest), is set over (below) or under (above) that cell: `1` over `N` is `1/N`, no spaces. A cell that opens with an operator run is never the one.
6. **Stacks.** The cells above or below the line, within 1.2 x leading of it, are grouped by the stretch of the line they stand over: two neighbours (left to right) belong together unless a run of the line's own text lies wholly between them. A group is a stack when some cell above and some cell below meet in x (overlap, or less than 0.9 em apart); a group of one side only is no stack.
7. **Writing.** A stack is `upper / lower`, each side its cells top to bottom and left to right within a level, parenthesised when a side has more than one cell (`(1/N n I(ab)/J(ab))`). The line's own cells stand either side of each stack by their centres; a stack that falls between two runs of one cell cuts the cell there. Cells with no partner across the line, and cells out of reach, are written after the line, in the rows' order (the brief's "after the base row"). An island with no stack and no limit returns null and keeps its rows.

**Departures from the brief, each small (the manager may overrule).**
- **Stacks are found by "no line text between"**, not by a row's x-span against a row's: the same on a clean fraction, and it holds a denominator the page cut in two cells (`N` ... `n I(hkl)/ I0(hkl)`) in one stack; a stack also needs an upper and a lower cell that really meet.
- **A limit is merged when the island has no stack too**; the island then reads on one line.
- **Two additional bail-outs** (two rows that hold `=`; a block with no letter and no `=`) are mine, for precision: a multi-line equation and a column of tick labels are not fractions.
- **The script-apart cell cut** (commit 2) is mine: in the owner's `TC`, the nested `1` falls 3.1 pt under the base line and 4.1 pt over the denominator's row, and the fold gives it to the nearer; one point further and it is a raised run of the denominator's row, glued to `N`'s cell, and the fraction read `N ¹ n …`. The fold's two windows (3.5 pt under a row, 7.5 pt over the next) cover every height between two tight rows, so such a `1` is always the base line's script or the denominator's, never a row of its own; a limit 5.5 pt under its sign, past both windows, is (tested).
- **Not done:** a limit the fold took into the operator's own row that stands before the operator in x order (wider than the sign) is not moved behind it; a numbered base row is lifted by `equationOf` before an island is looked for, so its numerator and denominator rows stay a separate stack (P9-09 §8.2, as before); a fraction bar drawn as text (none on the set).
