### Item 13 — Durable queue survives duplicate delivery, two workers, crash, 429, outage, timeout; stale job cannot overwrite new intent

**Verdict: PARTIAL.**

Evidence read directly:
- `web/supabase/migrations/20260924000600_dashboard_prepare_jobs.sql` (read in full): `claim_prepare_job()` uses `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` on rows that are `pending AND due` OR `leased AND lease_expires_at < now()` — the second clause is the crash-recovery path (a dead worker's expired lease is simply reclaimed by the next claim, no separate dead-worker detection needed). `heartbeat_prepare_job()`/`complete_prepare_job()`/`fail_prepare_job()` are all fenced on `lease_owner = p_lease_owner AND status = 'leased'`, so a worker whose lease was already reclaimed by someone else cannot complete/fail/heartbeat the job out from under the new holder. `enqueue_prepare_job()` is an atomic `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (owner_id, local_date) DO UPDATE` — the unique constraint IS the debounce mechanism (one pending-or-leased job per owner+date); a fresh enqueue for a still-pending/leased row updates `intent_version`/`next_attempt_at` in place without touching `status`/`attempts`/`lease`, so a stale job cannot overwrite what a NEWER enqueue asked for — the row itself is kept current. `fail_prepare_job()` moves to `'dead'` once `attempts >= max_attempts` (default 5, PROPOSED not sourced — the migration's own comment says so).
- **"Stale job cannot overwrite new intent"** — the fencing is deeper than the row-level debounce: `web/src/lib/dashboard/prepare-worker.ts` (per the round log, corroborated by the migration's own comment on `intent_version`) captures the FULL serialized intent at enqueue time and the worker "recomputes this fresh immediately before writing a batch and discards its own result on a mismatch rather than ever publish under a stale intent" — I did not re-open `prepare-worker.ts` myself this round (time-budgeted), so this specific claim is carried from the round log, not independently re-derived; I did independently confirm the migration's `intent_version text not null` column and its doc comment describing this exact design.
- The migration's own header comment states plainly: **"DB/RLS PROOF: BLOCKED. No isolated Supabase instance is available... the FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED claim... its correctness under genuinely concurrent connections can only be proven by a real Postgres."** I confirm this is still true in my own environment: no Docker, no Supabase CLI (per my own environment constraints, matching the task brief).
- `grep -rn "runPrepareJob\|claimPrepareJob\|drainPrepareQueue" app` (production routes only) → no hits. Nothing calls into this queue from any route. Who/what triggers it is explicitly USER decision #3 (readiness doc §8), unresolved.

Why PARTIAL: the queue's offline-provable properties (debounce, fencing, lease-expiry reclaim design, bounded retries, backoff math) are real and code-reviewed; the properties that specifically need "two workers, crash, 429, outage" (i.e., actual concurrent Postgres connections) are BLOCKED on missing DB infrastructure, and the whole mechanism is unreachable from any route today.
